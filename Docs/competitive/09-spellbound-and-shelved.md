## Premise & scope — genuinely different genre

This is **not** a first-person sorting game like the rest of the set. It's a cozy **isometric
idle/incremental** game: a wizard-academy library has vanished under its own books, and the player
hires red-hatted gnomes and unlocks shelves to let the tidying happen automatically, watching
rather than doing the sorting themselves.

## Mechanics

- Player role is management/economy, not manual pick-up-and-place: hire/upgrade gnomes, unlock
  shelves, spend money earned from customers.
- Customers visit and pay to pull books back off shelves (the "mess" is a deliberate, monetized
  game system, not just clutter to clear once).
- Core lever: an OPEN/CLOSED door sign — CLOSED lets gnomes tidy in peace with no income; OPEN
  brings in gold but lets the mess creep back. This is the whole tension/strategy of the game.

## Relevance to BookBoy

- Useful as a boundary case: it proves the "library restoration" *theme* is popular enough to
  support a completely different genre (idle/management) alongside the first-person physical
  version BookBoy is building. Not a direct mechanical competitor, but a signal the setting has
  broad appeal beyond one genre.
- Nothing here changes BookBoy's approach — flagging for awareness, not imitation. Revisit after
  it launches (Sep 7) to see reception.
