# Whisper of the House

*Not a shelf-sorter and not first person: included because it is the nearest proven example of a **story-driven tidy-up
game** (Kolby picked it from the 2026-10-19 candidate list as the closest to the Lamplit concept). Research date
2026-10-19, from the Steam store page and `appdetails`, the full Steam news feed (47 posts), **all 184 negative
reviews in every language** (92 English read in full; the top 22 Chinese negatives read), and 2,468 English positives
searched by keyword with the 14 most-upvoted read. **Nobody on the team has played it yet**, and no screenshots were
reviewed, so everything about how it looks and feels is second-hand from the store page and reviewers; the
personal-impressions section is blank.*

- **Steam:** [store.steampowered.com/app/2589500](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2589500/) (App ID 2589500)
- **Released:** August 27, 2025 (Windows and Mac; **no Linux**). A demo ran from Feb 24, 2025 and was removed at launch.
- **Price:** $14.99 ($13.49 launch week; 10% off in the Sep 30, 2025 sale; 20% "all-time low" in the Dec 2025 winter sale)
- **Reviews (2026-10-19):** Overwhelmingly Positive, **5,894 total, 96.9% positive** (5,710 / 184). English 2,560 (96.4%),
  Simplified Chinese 2,232 (97.1%). The store page's own recommendation count is 5,471.
- **Developer/publisher:** 元气弹工作室 (GD Studio), published with LoongGate. **Fewer than ten people** (their
  Nov 2025 post). Nine languages now (the first five were added Feb 2026). 89 Steam achievements, none missable, Steam Cloud,
  partial controller support.
- **Sales:** "150K copies sold" announced Nov 27, 2025, three months after launch.
- **DLC:** a free DLC, *Summer Whispers* (Aug 26 to 27, 2026), plus a $4.99 soundtrack (Sep 29, 2025).
- **Generative-AI disclosure:** none on the store page (the page has no AI-content field). One reviewer claims the
  summer DLC "uses AI" (Aug 29, 2026; 17 helpful votes). **Unverified**; the developer has not answered on Steam.

## Premise and structure (store page and reviewers)

You are the town's "all-around housekeeper" in **Whisper Town**, an open overworld you walk around (isometric, pixel art,
per the store page and reviewers). Townsfolk pay you to **move into homes, tidy cluttered rooms, set up shops, sort
keepsakes** and even find cats. Each job is a self-contained room or building ("level"). Sub-plots are told through the
people and the items; the mystery layer sits on top: a number, **42**, flickers over the museum, a rainbow, a ghost in the
well, tentacles in the cracks. Reviewers describe the premise as *Unpacking* + *Animal Crossing: Happy Home Designer* + a
creepy-cute town mystery.

- **Launch:** "15+ main levels, 4 unlockable homes (earned through hidden tasks)", **1,800+ furniture** items, and a daily
  refreshed **Ideal Home Showroom** (random themed layouts with daily tasks; 504 tasks and 206 floor layouts
  after the summer DLC).
- **Length:** main story roughly **7 to 10 hours** (a top positive review lists ~7 h to 100%, median English reviewer playtime
  9.8 h positive and 6.0 h negative), longer for decorators (several reviewers have 20 to 200 hours).
- **Hidden layer:** **42 "abnormalities"** (objects that glitch; click them to restore) collected for a gift from "the
  mysterious figure"; completing the set unlocks Custom Space (resizable floor and wall blocks, rename and describe
  your own items).
- **Verbs seen in reviews and patch notes:** carry things from boxes handed out one at a time by a **robot**, drag and
  place furniture (free-form, four-way rotation, recolor, stack), clean trash and stains, cook, bake, mix drinks
  (bartending), alchemy, a lottery or "gacha" for furniture, photo mode, presets that save and reload room layouts.
- **Post-launch content:** Winter update (Dec 18, 2025; Tasty Diner, Shadowed Courtyard, Snow King Festival), Spring Festival and
  Custom Space (Feb 10, 2026), free *Summer Whispers* DLC (4 rooms, fishing, BBQ parties, 340+ items, Aug 2026). Mod support and
  visiting other players' homes were floated in Nov 2025; not delivered as of Oct 2026.

## How its core "tidy" mechanic differs from ours

| | Whisper of the House | BookBoy / Lamplit |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Isometric overworld and rooms | First person |
| What counts as "correct" | **Mostly nothing.** Quests check that listed items are *in the room* or on a ghost target; otherwise any layout is accepted | A book either goes to its shelf or it does not (graded green/yellow/red) |
| Where the story lives | NPC dialogue, newspapers and request letters, item descriptions, abnormalities | Notes, finds, optional commissioned portraits (concept doc) |
| Pacing | Open town, quests unlock; story ends without warning | Seven days, each a wing, with a defined finale |
| Economy | Vouchers for furniture, plus a lottery; "pointless, you have thousands" (a review) | Upgrade points, librarian-craft abilities |

## What players love (English positives, 2,468 read by keyword, top 14 read in full)

Keyword counts, English positives: story/quest/mystery in 862 (35%), Unpacking in 579, decorating in 610, price/worth in 208, short/ending in 189.

1. **"Unpacking, but with more."** The most-upvoted positives (116, 115, 62 votes) say it scratches the *Unpacking* itch
   with more levels, more freedom and a **mystery in the background**. "More levels, the ability to design your own rooms,
   and/or underlying mysteries."
2. **The mystery is a reward for curiosity.** One reviewer (103 votes) notes the hidden layer is "so much fun to uncover" with
   no missable achievements; a negative reviewer still says two early levels "have incredible hidden secrets that truly make you
   feel smart for figuring them out". Hidden objects, clickable oddities, secret rooms and "pull the cord on the thing" gags are
   the beloved part.
3. **Small human stories land.** Reviewers single out emotional beats ("I miss my Grandpa. This game is worth playing for that
   little side story alone", 45 votes). The store art and music are praised even by negatives.
4. **No-stress placing and creative freedom** (62 votes: "without the stress of putting things in the 'right' place").
   Reviewers who dislike rules in tidy games like this part.
5. **Long tail of reviews.** 60 to 80 English positives a month eleven months after launch (Aug 2026: 67; Sep 2026: 79), with
   a free DLC in between. Compare most of the wave, which goes quiet in weeks.

## What players push back on (all 184 negatives; 92 English read in full, top Chinese read)

Negative share is 3.1% overall. In the English negatives (keyword matches, so approximate): **story, quests, ending or mystery in
45 of 92**; comparison to *Unpacking* in 29; too short or abrupt ending in 19; clutter, crowded rooms or missing storage in 18;
price in 18; no tutorial or goal guidance in 14; bugs in 12; loot box or gacha in 5; Steam Deck or controller in 4;
translation in 4.

1. **The story fizzles and ends without telling you** (the single biggest theme, and the one most relevant to Lamplit).
   "Quests just stop coming and the story fades out and you're left hanging" (18 votes; 78 hours played);
   "I thought I had a bug... I had just finished the main questline" (17); "ending was very lackluster to the point where
   I didn't even know it ended" (13); "the mystery/creepy aspect was lost halfway through and there were many unanswered
   questions"; "I don't want to play detective, I just want to organize" about vague newspaper clues (6). Players want a **marked
   ending** and answers to the questions the opening raises.
2. **"Not Unpacking."** 29 of 92 negatives compare it unfavourably: "In *Unpacking*, I felt connected to the world, like I was
   uncovering a story through the items themselves. This game has none of that" (24 votes). The core grievance is that
   **placement carries no meaning**: "it doesn't matter how you do it, throw it in a big stupid pile, it doesn't matter to the
   person you're organizing for, and it doesn't matter to the game world" (2); "Perfect Design: the townspeople love it. Abysmal
   Design: the townspeople love it" (15); a Chinese review (4 votes) says effort and sloppy placement get the same result. Also:
   "the game doesn't care where things are, the characters are flat" (12).
3. **Overcrowded rooms, not enough storage, and items arriving before the shelves.** Rooms get "too many clutter items per
   room, and too little storage spaces", drawers and cupboards are not functional, and the bakery is named repeatedly as
   the stressful level. A convenience-store level hands out items **before** the shelves exist, so players pile things on the floor
   (Chinese reviews, 5 to 6 votes). Players call out that this forces the order of play. Lesson for us: **supply of
   places must match supply of items.**
4. **Tedious one-at-a-time handling.** Taking each item out of the robot's box and trash one piece at a time, a "relocating
   keepsakes" quest where you can carry one item per trip (10+ trips, 7 votes, Chinese), and slow trash cleaning.
5. **Short for the price.** "Too short for the price" (7), "10 hours, credits at 89%" (2), "6 hours for $54 CNY" (4, Chinese).
6. **Weak guidance**: no tutorial, hard to tell where to go or how to get more items (14 reviews).
7. **Tone clash and removed darkness.** The demo was darker and the full game softened it ("I've heard the demo was more creepy
   and mysterious than this", 3; "spookiness felt thrown in", 12). A Chinese review (20 votes, the top Chinese negative) says the
   *demo* contained a captivity-and-voyeurism plot involving a female NPC, and that a replacement still contained voyeur
   elements; the developers did publicly apologise (Feb 26, 2025) for "inappropriate content" and replaced the room. That
   reviewer's wording of the replacement is unverified. Two English reviewers dislike the occult imagery (pentagram, tarot).
8. **Bugs, mostly at launch and then softlocks since:** the hospital towels bug, unclickable "Grandpa" mission, items lost when
   autosave fired while holding them, lost items in crafting, Mac controls broken, Steam Deck problems, rooms that could not be
   finished. The developers answered with a "Lost Items Reset" button and a "Reset Level" function; a Chinese reviewer (10 votes)
   complains they tell players to reset instead of fixing.
9. **Translation and writing.** Untranslated Chinese text at launch (English review, 10 votes, "lose out from story bits"),
   odd or overwritten item descriptions, "childish approach to writing" (1).
10. **Trust and monetisation:** one **stolen-art accusation** (artists named; the dev log shows "replaced problematic and
    questionable assets" on Sep 2 and Sep 5, 2025), a **loot-box complaint** (5 reviews: "lootbox in a customization game is
    diabolical"), and the single **AI accusation about the free DLC** (Aug 2026). It has not changed the score.
11. **Save sync.** Many files from free placement cause Steam Cloud conflicts; the dev capped manual saves at four and told
    players to turn cloud saves off.

## Why it matters for Lamplit (what to take, and what to avoid)

These are the points I'd put in front of design; none is built yet, and they follow the evidence above.

1. **Mark the ending, and answer the opening questions.** The most common complaint among a 96.9% title is that the story
   stops silently and some questions never resolve. Lamplit's seven days with a defined last day already avoids the first
   half; keep the second by making every mystery thread seeded in Day 1 pay off by Day 7, and show an explicit "last
   room" state. Also an easy fix they lack: a last-day cue before the final task.
2. **Let placement mean something to the story.** The *Unpacking* comparison fails here because the game does not react to
   where things go. In Lamplit, a correctly shelved set already matters; the narrative should also react to it (a note appears
   once a particular wing is sorted, a find goes on the desk). Worth stating in the concept doc: "the shelves are the evidence."
3. **Do not hand out items before there is somewhere to put them.** Shelf capacity must match item supply every room. We
   already note this for the sorting wave; Whisper shows the same failure in a story game (the convenience store, the bakery).
4. **Optional mystery has value, but hints must be legible.** The hidden layer (42 abnormalities, secret rooms) is loved, yet "vague
   newspaper clues" are the one place reviewers say they didn't want to play detective. Optional finds with a visible counter
   (their abnormality collection) beat vague hints.
5. **Keep the tone consistent.** The demo-to-launch softening and the "occult" objections show the cost of a tone swing. Lamplit
   is explicitly no-magic and no-horror; keep every note in the same cozy-mystery voice.
6. **Price versus length.** $14.99 for 7 to 10 hours drew "too short" in 19 of 92 English negatives despite a 96.9% score.
   A $10 to $15 price needs enough hours or replay value (Whisper's answer was seasonal updates and a showroom).
7. **Free-placement save size.** If Lamplit ever lets players place freely, per-object saves bloat; Whisper had to cap slots and
   advise against cloud saves. Our books-on-shelves model stores far less.
8. **Don't pitch as "Unpacking-like" unless the design delivers it.** A significant share of the 3.1% negatives are people who came
   expecting *Unpacking* and did not get it ("If you came here because of Unpacking, move along").
9. **Dev communication is cheap and worked.** A tiny team posted a patch note every few days for the first month and a
   progress report; their score survived a rough launch (bugs, stolen-art accusation, demo controversy).
