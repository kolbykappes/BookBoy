## Premise and scale

> "Arthur ran this shop for thirty years. Now it's yours. Last night his grandson Theo wanted to play with every game, just once,
> while Arthur fell asleep in the armchair. Theo didn't. Put every game back where it belongs and the shop is ready to open its doors again."

The opening is a **hand-written welcome letter** (screenshot 2, signed "— Arthur": "Dear new keeper… Every game has its place on
the shelves. Put them all back, and the shop is ready to open again. Welcome home."). That letter is the whole story the page
promises, but it is a real narrative wrapper: an inherited shop, a departing relative, a child's mess.

- **3,816 boxes**: **318 board games × 12 copies**, each with "its own parody cover". Shelved copies number 12 per game.
- **15 categories on two floors** (Kids, Party & Words, Fantasy & Dungeons, Sci-Fi & Space, Mystery & Spies, Farm & Village,
  City & Industry, War & History, Horror, Animals…).
- **Themed nooks:** a sunny greenhouse, a candle-lit horror room, a detective's office and a reading loft.
- **No timers, no pressure; save any time.**

## Mechanics and abilities (store page, screenshots)

- **Verbs:** pick up copies, **carry a wobbly stack** and cycle through it with the mouse wheel, **throw** a box (Q), run
  (Shift), zoom, open the skill book (Tab). The stack counter reads like **3/13, 2/7, 4/7**: capacity grows with a skill.
- **Set completion reward:** "fill a section with all twelve copies and watch it light up in a **cascade of little boxes**."
- **HUD:** an **XP bar ("10 XP to next skill point")**, a **count of games finished (e.g. 30/318)** and a **count of boxes
  (e.g. 547/3816)**. A **right-hand list shows the titles and categories of the held stack** (screenshot 1:
  "Stardoze Valley · Farm & Village" three times). A **shop map on a lectern by the stairs** (screenshot 5).
- **Shelves** carry category signs ("WAR & HISTORY", "FARM & VILLAGE", "HORROR") and **small per-game price-tag labels**
  under each bay.
- **XP per game shelved** gives skill points. **Seven skills, four active (keys 1 to 4), three passive:**

| Skill | Type | Effect (store text and screenshot) |
|---|---|---|
| Copy Sense | active (1) | Every loose copy of the game you are holding glows with an outline, even behind shelves and walls. **Lv 1/5: cooldown 45 s → 40 s, active 10 s → 13 s** (screenshot 4) |
| Magnet Hands | active | Pulls nearby copies straight to you |
| Quick Stack | active | Fills a shelf in one go |
| Smart Stack | active | Sorts your stack as you look at the shelves |
| Passives (3) | passive | Faster running, carrying more games at once, and a third unnamed (all show Lv 1 to Max) |

The skill menu is a **hardbound book with a ribbon and brass corners** (Tab), a cozy re-skin like Witch Cabin's ledger.

## Why it matters to us

1. **The nearest match to Lamplit's frame in this wave: an inherited shop, a departing older relative, a letter that sets the
   task.** It spends one letter on the story and moves to shelving. Lamplit's Uncle Wilfred does the same with more substance.
   Worth noting how little narrative the competition currently carries: **one letter**.
2. **Set-completion celebration.** A cascade of boxes when a 12-copy set is completed, the per-game "games finished" counter
   (30/318) and the per-box counter: two progress layers. Compare our green/yellow/red grading and shelf glow.
3. **A HUD list of the held stack's destinations** (title + category). The most direct answer yet to the "which shelf?"
   problem, and cheaper than a spell. We have a held-stack panel; this shows the destination category beside each title.
4. **Skill cooldowns and durations are tuned in tiny steps** (45 s → 40 s, 10 s → 13 s). Same as Witch Cabin: level 2 barely
   changes anything, which reviewers will notice.
5. **Voiced letter in 12 languages**: full audio in all twelve is unusual and cheap marketing for a tiny game, but it also means
   twelve recordings to keep consistent.
6. **Risks I can see (guesses, not sources):** 318 unique parody covers is a lot of art for a new studio with no disclosed
   process, so an AI accusation is plausible under the pattern in our review tracker; parody names of real games carry
   trademark and humour-misfire risk; no controller support listed.
