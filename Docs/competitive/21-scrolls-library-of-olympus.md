## Premise and scale

A divine library whose gods "put their scrolls away… apparently beneath them." **3,048 scrolls in 400 collections across 24
categories** (astronomy, geography, history, epic poetry and so on). The page says "study each scroll's **title, colour,
emblem, and Roman numeral**": a collection is recognised by its emblem, and the numeral is its order on the shelf. The
announcement says "3,048 scrolls across **400 shelves**", while the store page says 400 *collections*; the in-game
ledger (screenshot 2) shows both a Shelves counter and a Collections counter at 0 / 400, so each collection seems to be one
shelf. About 7.6 scrolls per collection; the held-scroll panel in the screenshots reads "Volume 1 of 10" for one set, so
sets vary in size.

There is no story beyond the joke premise. A companion **owl** (pet it, or have it fetch scrolls) is the only character.

## Mechanics and abilities (store text, unplayed)

- **Verbs:** pick up, **rotate and inspect** a scroll in your hands (a dedicated "Arcane Inspection" mode, screenshot 2),
  carry a stack, set a collection aside, **toss a scroll toward a sorting pile**, and **move, insert and swap already
  shelved volumes** (the Still Sorting and Librarian style of gap-and-reorder).
- **Ordering rule:** group by collection (emblem), then Roman-numeral order within the shelf. The shelf is a single collection
  and the order matters, like BookBoy's series and volume rule.
- **Upgrades:** "Level up by placing scrolls correctly" (XP per correct placement, as in Megastore 1.0.6 and Magic Mess).
  Abilities named on the page:

| Ability | Effect (as described) |
|---|---|
| Sort | Scrolls in your hands arrange themselves |
| Shelf Guide | Reveals the rightful shelf of a carried scroll |
| Insight | Uncovers nearby volumes belonging to the same collection |
| Auto-Shelve | Sends matching scrolls straight to their places |
| Assemble | Summons scattered volumes of a collection into your hands (compare Sort of Magic's Assemble) |
| Teleport | Puts you in front of the matching bookcase of your held scroll |
| Passives | Carry capacity, move speed, reach height |

  The ladder follows the standard find, collect, place, automate pattern. No prices, levels or cooldowns are shown.
- **Co-op:** divide collections, find each other's missing volumes, shared save.

body and a metallic emblem cap**. Scale is "grand hall", a deliberate contrast with Lamplit's small-room direction.

## How it differs from, and resembles, Lamplit

| | SCROLLS | Lamplit |
|---|---|---|
| Item | Cylindrical scrolls with emblem and numeral | Tomes with printed covers, spine number and set icon |
| Ordering | Collection by emblem then numeral | Set by group then volume number |
| Space | A god-sized hall, 400 shelves | A small town library, a few rooms |
| Story | None (jokes) | Notes, finds, a family arc |
| Aid ladder | Sort, Shelf Guide, Insight, Auto-Shelve, Assemble, Teleport | Librarian-craft abilities, no magic |
| Co-op | Up to 4 | Single-player |

## Why it matters to us

1. **A second "ledger" screen.** Witch Cabin's "Enchanted Ledger" and SCROLLS's "Restoration Ledger" are two independent
   uses of a framed in-world progress panel. A shared pattern: stats shown as a ledger (shelves, items, sets, categories) rather
   than a bar. A good fit for Lamplit's "cataloguing" feel.
2. **Emblem plus numeral is the same two-cue approach we already settled** (icon plus number on the spine). Worth watching
   how legible the emblem is at distance once there is a demo.
3. **The mix of "inspect and rotate" with shelving.** Examining an item in your hands is a charm in a library setting
   and is absent from most of the wave.
4. **Speed to market.** A tidy-wave game revealed on Oct 14 and shipping Nov 1 shows how fast competitors can
   appear; it is a reason not to count on novelty of the setting.
5. **Risks I can see:** no AI disclosure (the page says "handcrafted", which the Oct 17 refresh warns can become a liability),
   a heavier hardware floor, a brand new studio, and the grand-hall scale will make shelf and item legibility hard.
