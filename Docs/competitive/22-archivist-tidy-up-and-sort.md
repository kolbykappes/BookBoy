## Premise and structure

A first-person **archive worker** brings order to rooms of misplaced documents. It is the only entry in this batch with an
explicit **work-day structure and office fiction**, in two modes:

- **Sandbox Mode:** choose a level and get a series of archive tasks. "Work instructions … may vary from level to level":
  **sort by date, by number, by category, or by several parameters at once.** Documents are in boxes or scattered.
  Rewards arrive as perks: "faster searching, quicker document placement, automatic sorting".
- **Campaign Mode:** "a series of short sessions structured around working days." Each day adds tasks: **sort incoming
  documents, find and submit requested files, check and edit records, clean the workspace, fix mistakes made by other employees.**
  This is the nearest thing in the wave to a day-by-day structure like Lamplit's seven days.

Counts: the dev's own post shows a pile of "roughly 5,000 documents"; the demo HUD in the screenshots reads **"0 / 960 in
Place"** for one level. There is **no stated global item count** on the page.

## Mechanics (store page, demo patch notes, screenshots)

- **Verbs (the instruction panel in screenshot 2):** place a document on a shelf, pick up a document from a shelf, **shift
  documents on a shelf**, **swap a held document with a shelf document**, **sort the documents in your hand**. Also carry
  boxes, push a **cart** (hold F to release it), climb **ladders**, restart a level, reorganise shelf rows and "improve the
  layout".
- **Order rules shown in HUD:** a **Progress bar (%)**, a **count of documents in place**, and a separate **"100% Order"** bar
  (screenshots 5 and 6): the game scores *placement* and *ordering within the shelf* separately. That is the closest of any
  competitor to our "right shelf" versus "right slot" grading.
- **Difficulty:** the Office level now has **three difficulty options**, each a different task with the same theme (Sep 12 patch).
- **Perks:** speed-ups and auto-sorting; no names, prices or numbers published.

## Why it matters to us

1. **A real ordering rule, graded in two tiers.** Placement and order tracked separately (Progress versus Order). The Mall
   and Still Sorting files show how much players care that the rule is correct.
2. **Multiple sort keys per level** (date, number, category, several at once) is the model for variants like alphabetical,
   by author, and by date for our book rooms.
3. **A working-day frame without a story.** Campaign Mode proves a small team can bolt "tasks from the boss, fix a coworker's
   mistake" onto a sorter. Lamplit's seven days could borrow the *file a requested document* task; note it is not a narrative.
4. **Levels have a proper ending and a stats screen** was added after demo feedback: the same lesson as Whisper's
   "story ends without telling you". Do it from the start.
5. **Early warning signs:** a solo developer, a save reset in the demo, floating documents in piles, similar label colours. The
   colour-similarity item matches what sank Sort of Magic (indistinguishable books).
6. **No controller support** on the page, a gap next to Scrolls and Her Toys.
