## Premise and scale

> "Long ago, a little girl named Alice lived in a grand old mansion full of toys. Now the house is silent and dusty, and her toys
> are everywhere... Alice is still here, a gentle ghost who wants nothing more than to see her room in order again."

- **Three floors, 20 hand-furnished rooms**: a moonlit nursery, a dusty library, a music room, a cluttered attic, a
  "Blue Bedroom", a "Parlor" and others (shown as room names on the screen).
- **100 kinds of toys.** "More than 7,000 are scattered across the house" so players need to find **6,000** and "the last
  few of each kind are never impossible to find". A top-left counter reads **TOYS 124/6000** in the screenshots.
- **The Lobby of Toys**: "a great hall lined with **100 shelves**", **60 toys per shelf**, so each shelf is one toy type
  (100 × 60 = 6,000). Counters on shelves read like "46/60" and "55/60".
- **Alice follows you** through the house, waits when you stop, and in the lobby "will walk you straight to its shelf".
  The only narrative on the page is the backstory; there is no mention of notes, a plot or an ending.

## Mechanics (store page and screenshots)

- **A two-stage loop that no other game in this batch has: hunt, then shelve.** Dig toys out of **physics-driven heaps that
  "tumble when you dig in"**, open doors and drawers, lift chairs, and carry toys to the lobby. It is an escape-room /
  hidden-object hunt first, a sorter second.
- **Inventory:** a "toy-holding" system. A bottom-line prompt reads "Holding Classic Bear **x24** · [F / LB] next toy ·
  [Q / RB] put away". Toys are carried as **typed stacks** by kind rather than as individual objects, and you cycle between kinds.
  An "inventory" bar with a "to open" counter, a **minimap** (floor-labelled) and an inventory screen tracking each collection.
- **Placement help:** hold a toy up and **its empty places glow on its shelf "like little ghosts"** (screenshot 5: glowing
  outlines of bears in the empty slots; one slot highlighted in yellow). A shelf label with **distance ("Plush Unicorn shelf, 18 m")**
  floats above each shelf (screenshot 4).
- **No combat**, no timer mentioned, three save slots. No ability tree or XP is mentioned.

## Why it matters to us

1. **It adds a "search" half to the loop.** Collecting items from heaps and rooms, then shelving, solves the pacing problem
   that pure shelving has, and the dev's design note about **7,000 toys for 6,000 required** ("the last few of each kind are never
   impossible to find") is a clean fix for the unfinishable-run complaint in Mall and Tidy Up History. Worth borrowing for
   Lamplit's finds, in a smaller way.
2. **Ghost-slot preview on the shelf** is the same pattern as Librarian, Witch Cabin and SCROLLS: three independent
   confirmations that a green or ghost slot is now table stakes.
3. **A companion who walks you to the shelf.** A narrative character as a hint device is a better answer than a glowing arrow.
   Compare SCROLLS's owl, DinoBones' cat, and Lamplit's lamp.
4. **Typed stacks you cycle through (F / LB)** is a simple carry model for many items: no grid, matches our "no inventory
   grids" rule.
5. **Tone risk (a guess, supported by Whisper's reviews).** Whisper's "spookiness felt thrown in" and occult complaints show the
   cost of a tone swing. This store page promises cozy but is tagged Horror, Lovecraftian, Dark Humor; either the tags are
   wrong or the tone will be mixed. A cozy audience may refund over a jump scare.
6. **Counts are small and exact** (100 shelves, 60 each, 6,000 required): easy to understand, and no shelf-capacity mismatch
   as in Megastore's alphabet shelf.
