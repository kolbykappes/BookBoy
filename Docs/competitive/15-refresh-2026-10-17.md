# Competitive Refresh — 2026-10-17

Second pass over the "Tidy Up" wave, six weeks after the 2026-09-06 baseline. Three jobs: (1) patch notes
and updates for the games already tracked, (2) what reviewers are pushing back on now, (3) a first-pass
sweep for games we haven't catalogued. **No new deep dives yet** — section 4 is a shortlist for the
user to pick from.

**Method / caveats**
- Review counts and scores: Steam `appreviews` API, all languages, all time (same method as the baseline).
- Pushback: every English-language *negative* review posted since 2026-09-06 for the original 8 titles
  (236 reviews), and the 10-17 most-upvoted negatives for the 6 titles that have launched since (a skim,
  not a full read). "AI-mentioning" counts are keyword matches, so treat them as approximate.
- Patch notes: Steam's `ISteamNews` feed only. Anything a dev posts only on Discord is invisible here.
- Discovery: Steam store search, filtered by name, then checked for release date and traction. Several
  queries were rate-limited and re-run; the name filter will have dropped some titles. **The list in
  section 4 is a sample of a much bigger flood (roughly 100+ tidy-up-style releases since mid-August,
  most under $6 with under 50 reviews), not a complete census.**

### Notable patch-note content

- **Megastore** was the most active. 1.0.6 changed progression so **XP is earned per correct placement
  instead of per completed shelf** (75% of a shelf's XP is paid out as items go on, 25% on completion);
  the dev polled their Discord first (86% in favour) because scattered-item maps made points feel slow.
  Electronics Store shipped Sep 18 with 6,203 items, no timed or no-skill achievements, and **large

  Workshop sharing and co-op on custom maps**, closed playtest "coming soon."
- **Plushy Store** answered its core complaint directly: Patch 5 (Sep 6) now shows wrong placements from
  the *first* wrong plushy in Challenge Mode, not after four. Sep 30 update added multiplayer, character
  customization, Twitch chat integration, a new section, and a "plush" fabric material. Score is up
  about 3 points but still Mixed.
- **Tidy Up History** shipped with progression softlocks (keys gated behind abilities players hadn't
  unlocked), then patched: raised the level requirement for a late skill, made keys findable by audio,
  turned off camera bobbing, raised the camera, added FOV, added a "Hints & Cheats" menu, and (1.0.7)
  made the helper abilities and the entire skill tree **skippable**.
- **Black Friday Janitor** v1.0.4: item colors now generate in **even distribution** so every color group
  fills a shelf cleanly (the first-launch complaint was uneven color counts). v1.1.0 added a
  **"Chaos Mode"** (items spawn anywhere) and an **"all skills unlocked"** mode, both unlocked after one
  full clear. Dev is evaluating multiplayer.
- **Wand & Order**: free **Megamap** (Oct 8) joining all three zones, with an option to play without
  abilities or cleaning tasks. Console ports in progress, PlayStation shipped Oct 15.
- **DinoBones**: 36-fix update Sep 27; Oct 3 added **three save slots**, an **anatomical mode** (must place

- **Spellbound & Shelved** added toggles to hide the activity chat and show a "books on floor" counter.
- **Sort Them Ducks** and **Mall** are the stagnation cases. Ducks reviews from Oct still describe

## 2. What reviewers are pushing back on now (negatives since 2026-09-06)

New English negatives: Librarian 88, Sort Them Ducks 33, Mall 32, Supermarket Chaos 23, Megastore 17,
Shelve the Potions 11, Shelves and Sorcery 7, Plushy 6. In rough order of weight:

1. **AI trust is still the biggest single driver, and the sharpest new form is "you said no AI."**
   Librarian ~56 of 88 and Mall ~17 of 32 mention AI. The pattern has shifted from "undisclosed" to
   **"disclosure added after I bought it"** (Librarian) and **"claimed none, found some"**. DinoBones is the
   cautionary tale: its page said no AI in gameplay, reviewers spotted generated-looking wall art and the
   map, and the top negative review has **~500 helpful votes**; most of its ~45 negatives are about this.
   Megastore lost goodwill when the Electronics Store brought what players read as generated game-cover art
   after people had already bought. Black Friday Janitor, whose dev says no AI was used, drew reviews arguing
   its bought asset packs look generated. **Lesson: a "no AI" claim is a liability unless every visible asset can back it,
   including posters, covers and UI art.** Shelve the Potions again has almost none of this.
2. **Motion sickness / camera comfort is a new, cross-genre complaint.** Named in Shelves and Sorcery
   (top negative, 24 votes, motion blur), Mall (23 votes), Black Friday Janitor (several, even with blur at
   0%), Paver (a group of friends "laid on the floor for hours"), Supermarket Chaos, and Tidy Up History
   (camera bob, camera too low). Devs responded with FOV sliders, bob toggles and higher cameras.
3. **Padding and near-duplicate items.** Supermarket Chaos's last map hits 9,999 items by repeating
   hundreds of tiny near-identical packages; Shelves and Sorcery has a dozen berry types differing only in
   shade; Sort Them Ducks has two shelves and 20 copies per duck; Wand & Order's items "all look the same,
   different colour"; Megastore and Supermarket Chaos reuse assets.
4. **Mechanic exhausted early** (carries over from the baseline): Shelve the Potions' puzzles are "done" by
   the second or third color with roughly 80% of the game left; Spellbound is ~2 hours of waiting.
5. **Short, with no replay path.** Tidy Up History (~3 hrs), Paver (one map, 90 min co-op), Wand & Order
   ("very short"). Players ask for a free-play or new-game-plus mode with abilities already unlocked,
   which Black Friday Janitor then shipped.
6. **Softlocks and unfinishable runs**: Tidy Up History's gated keys, Mall's missing items, Potions stuck
   between shelves, Megastore co-op disconnect eating a player's inventory, Wand & Order save resets
   (one reviewer with 221 hours), Black Friday Janitor tasks that don't appear.
7. **Domain logic that's wrong or doesn't fit.** Megastore's first level has **no room for the last two
   alphabet letters on the shelf** (top Megastore negative, **102 votes**). Wand & Order's tarot isn't in
   the canonical order, and a French-language option leaves the items untranslated. Shelve the Potions
   makes helper spells too easy to trigger by accident. **For BookBoy: shelf capacity must match the
   ordering rule exactly, and any real-world ordering we claim must be right.**
8. **Feel of the core interaction**: Black Friday Janitor's grab needs repeated clicks; Paver's animations
   block the next pickup; Ducks can't place with a gamepad; Tidy Up History's movement "limps."
9. **Achievement/progression fairness.** Megastore removed timed and no-skill achievements for the new map,
   then a Discord poll partly reversed it and a buyer who bought on the promise complained. Plushy's
   post-launch achievements were called ridiculous.
10. **Other**: a Supermarket Chaos reviewer lost an hour of progress because the *Load* screen uses the
    floppy-disk icon (save-slot UI clarity); culturally insensitive duck designs still show up in Ducks
    reviews through October; "shovelware / orgslop / clone" is a standard phrase now.

## 3. Interesting ideas worth stealing or testing

Ordered roughly by relevance to BookBoy's current design (timed runs, points per finished shelf, tech tree,
multi-carry):

1. **Pay progress per placement, not per finished shelf** (Megastore 1.0.6). Our points-per-finished-shelf
   design has the same weakness Megastore had to fix; a hybrid (most on placement, bonus on completion) kept
   total progression unchanged.
2. **Skills that recharge from placements, not a clock** (Mall; praised even by negative reviewers).
   Contrast Shelves and Sorcery's long cooldown, which a reviewer called out.
3. **Opt-out of the tech tree**: skippable ability screen and toggles (Tidy Up History), "play without
   abilities" (Wand & Order), "all skills unlocked" (Black Friday Janitor). Players want both pure and
   power-fantasy runs.
4. **Rule-based difficulty chosen at New Game** (DinoBones): free order vs anatomical order, hint proximity
   on/off, hint window on/off, plus a difficulty estimate. For books: strict (alphabetical or Dewey)
   vs by-category.
5. **Even color/category distribution** so groups always fill shelves (Black Friday Janitor).
6. **Map reshuffle with a spread dial** (Megastore proposal): same level, items scattered tight or wide.
7. **Level editor + Steam Workshop + co-op on custom maps** (Megastore, previewed Oct 16).
8. **Companion and photo mode** (DinoBones' museum cat and Polaroid camera; ~12,000 of ~20,000 early players
   found the secret camera). Low-cost warmth that reviewers mention positively.
9. **Proximity cue for hidden collectibles** (coins glint within two meters; last two show through walls).
10. **Large items with distinct carry poses, weight and collision** (Megastore).
11. **Streamer hooks**: Twitch chat integration (Plushy).
12. **Narrative wrappers**: Neat! (eight client stories), Deleted Wiki (2007 fan-wiki restoration, 10,324
    items, drag-and-drop), Museologist (one week to save the museum). Note Neat! is only 74%, so story alone
    doesn't carry it.
13. **Mechanics that aren't "match the picture"**: Brick by Brick (follow a building plan; 18,490 bricks;
    up to 5-player co-op), Royal Cartographer (jigsaw map fragments), Museologist (fragment matching),
    Tidy Up the Flower Shop (paint the baskets), Sort Labs (order shapes small to large), Liminal Sorting
    (horror-adjacent with timed minigames).
14. **Save UX**: multiple slots, cloud saves, background autosave, and a mismatch-proof Save/Load icon
    set. DinoBones' "no autosave by design" drew complaints.
