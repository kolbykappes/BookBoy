# 25. Steam release path for a small Unity game

Researched 2026-10-18 by a web-research agent (no Unity involved). Today is 2026-10-18; every Steamworks page below was fetched live on that date unless tagged otherwise.
Tags: **[fetched]** = I read the page itself via WebFetch (note that WebFetch summarises the page with a small model, so exact wording can be slightly paraphrased); **[search-only]** = from search-result summaries or third-party sites, not confirmed on a primary page; **[derived]** = my inference from fetched facts; **UNSOURCED GUESS** = from memory or judgement, no source read.
Market context comes from `Docs/competitive/README.md`, `review-tracker.md` and `15-refresh-2026-10-17.md` (read-only).

## 1. Question

What does it actually take to ship BookBoy (a first-person cozy book-sorting game, Unity 6.6 URP, Windows PC, with saves, multiple zones, per-zone leaderboards) on Steam as a first-time, solo/small developer, in what order, at what cost, and what rules (store review, AI disclosure, Unity licence, refunds, pricing) will shape the plan? Achievements and controller support are only touched on, as later work.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Steamworks partner signup, fee, paperwork, lead time

- The fee is **$100 USD per product**, non-refundable, payable by any Steam-supported method except Steam Wallet. It is **recouped from a later payment once the product reaches $1,000 Adjusted Gross Revenue** (Steam store plus in-app). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect
- What the fee "covers": the right to create and distribute that one app (App ID, Steamworks access, store page, builds, review). It is not a hosting or per-sale fee; there is no further listing fee in the pages I read. [derived from https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding]
- Onboarding order: sign the digital paperwork (an NDA and the Steam Distribution Agreement), pay the fee, complete bank + tax + identity verification, then Steamworks access opens. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding
- Paperwork needs: legal name and company form matching bank and tax documents ("Don't use a DBA or nickname"); individuals can onboard as a "Sole Proprietorship"; bank routing + account number + bank address; the account-holder name must match the name given at onboarding; a company without a business bank account must open one first. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding and https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect
- Tax: US persons give W-9-style information; non-US persons give W-8-style information, and a treaty can reduce the default 30% US-source withholding. The tax interview takes about 5 to 10 minutes and cannot be saved partway. Third-party verification "may take 2-7 business days"; IRS TIN validation can lag, and a recently issued TIN "up to 30 days". [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/faq
- Forming a legal entity is optional ("entirely up to you"); individuals can apply for an EIN or ITIN. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/faq
- **Lead time for paperwork: "15-30 days to complete"** per Valve's Steam Direct page. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect . The onboarding page adds that tax verification by a third-party service "can take up to 30 days", and you cannot change tax info until it finishes. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding
- **The waiting period after paying the fee disagrees between two Valve pages.** Steam Direct says the game cannot release until **30 days** after the fee is paid; the onboarding page says **21 days**. Both say this applies to "your first few titles". Plan for 30. [fetched, sources disagree] https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect vs https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding
- Payment: Valve pays sales "30 days after they are made, usually at the end of the month" (February sales paid end of March); payment only issues if prior-month sales exceed **$100**; ACH for US banks, USD SWIFT otherwise. The near-real-time Sales & Activations numbers exclude refunds, chargebacks and taxes; the monthly report is the true payout. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting
- Revenue share: Steam's default cut is **30%**, dropping to 25% and then 20% at higher per-game revenue. The pages on partner.steamgames.com that I fetched did not state this. Sources disagree on whether the lower rates apply only to revenue above each threshold (bracketed) or to all later sales. Irrelevant at our scale (thresholds are $10M and $50M). [search-only] https://www.neowin.net/news/valve-introduces-new-revenue-share-tiers-for-steam-promising-bigger-cuts-for-studios/ (the fetch of that page returned HTTP 403; the claim is from search summaries).

### 2.2 Release timeline and review

- **Coming Soon page must be public "for at least 2 weeks" before release** (applies to new products). Submit it for review at least **7 business days** before you want it live (the releasing page says "7 days"; the review-process page says "7 business days"; treat as 7 business days). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon , https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing , https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process
- Review of the store page typically takes **3-5 business days**; the build is a separate review, also 3-5 business days, and **the store page must be submitted before the build**. Upload a "mostly final" build to the default branch; updates after approval need no re-review. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process
- What reviewers check: the page shows only launch content (no planned features unless clearly marked "not yet released"); capsule images carry a readable title/logo; screenshots show only gameplay (no concept art, pre-rendered stills, awards or marketing copy); the description is coherent and has no external links; every feature listed on the page exists in the build; the game starts on each listed OS; in-game transactions use Steam Wallet. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process
- Approved games do not release themselves: you press "Release App". You need both "Publish app changes to Steam" and "Manage pricing and discounts" permissions. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing
- The competitive refresh records two titles slipping their release by 3 to 5 days "waiting on Steam review" (Wand & Order, DinoBones). [fetched locally] Docs/competitive/15-refresh-2026-10-17.md. So leave slack.
- Release-date display: pick exact date, month+year, quarter, year, or none ("Coming Soon"); you must still enter an exact intended date that players do not see; the date **cannot be changed in the last two weeks before it**. Games sort as if released on the last day of the displayed period; no-date games sort behind dated ones. Weekend release is possible but not recommended (no Steam support on weekends). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/release_dates
- **Wishlists:** Valve says there is no minimum wishlist count needed for Steam to show a game, no formula predicts sales from wishlists, and "Players who wishlist your game two years ahead of release can be just as likely to purchase" as late ones (so a long Coming Soon runway has "no strong drawback"). Wishlisters get an email/mobile push at release; wishlist data in the Sales & Activation portal lags a day. Wishlists also feed upcoming lists and personalised featuring. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon
- Valve advice on when to publish the Coming Soon page: once art direction and core features are fairly settled, since heavy changes after people wishlist can confuse them. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon
- Third-party wishlist rules of thumb: about **7,000** wishlists at launch is "common" for a commercially serious release, 5,000 as a floor; day-one wishlist-to-sale conversion quoted at roughly 12% to 25%; lifetime about 19% in 12 months. These come from marketing blogs/tool vendors, explicitly not Valve numbers ("Valve has never published a wishlist target"), and they disagree with each other. [search-only] https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch , https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/how-many-steam-wishlists-do-you-need
- Launch visibility: a full release enters the New Releases queue, which each time prioritises titles with the fewest views since release; New & Trending is a performance-based placement and can be pushed off when many popular products release at once. Reviews below 40% positive make a game less likely to be featured. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/visibility
- **Demos:** a demo is a **separate App ID** (type Demo, linked to the base game) with its own depots and builds. A separate demo page needs a trailer and at least 5 demo-specific screenshots. Wishlisters can be notified **once**, within two weeks of the demo's first playable release, and there is a **2-week cooldown** between wishlist emails per app (plan the demo release at least two weeks away from a discount or the full release). Valve recommends disabling achievements in the demo and keeping saves in Steam Cloud. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist
- **Steam Next Fest dates (official):** Oct 19-26 2026, **Feb 22-Mar 1 2027**, **Jun 14-21 2027**. Eligibility: unreleased game, public base-game store page, a publicly playable demo at the start, will not release before the edition ends, **a title may only ever join one Next Fest**, not a prologue of a released game. Demo build review must be submitted **5 weeks** before the fest (if releasing before the Press Preview) or **3 weeks** before (if releasing before the fest starts). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest . Registration deadlines are not on those pages; a third-party summary says the October 2026 registration closed Aug 31 (about seven weeks before the event). [search-only] https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-october-2026 . Third-party sources disagree with each other on the October demo-review deadline (Sep 21 / Sep 28 / Oct 5), so check the registration page when it opens.
- Other events that accept Coming Soon games with demos: Shop Keeper Fest (Jan 25-Feb 1 2027), Witch Fest (Apr 22-26 2027), among others. Seasonal sales (Winter Dec 17 2026-Jan 4 2027, Spring Mar 18-25 2027, Summer Jun 24-Jul 8 2027) require the game to have been released at least **30 days** before the event starts. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events . Whether BookBoy's theme fits a themed fest is a judgement call (project memory says the real game concept has no magic, so Witch Fest likely does not fit). [derived]

- Capsule text rule: "Do not include quotes or other strings of text beyond the title of your game" on Header, Main and Vertical capsules. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/standard
- **Trailer:** .mov/.wmv/.mp4, H.264 + AAC preferred, highest resolution you have up to 1920x1080 at 30 or 60 fps, 5,000+ kbps; no stated length or size limit. Valve says make the first trailer gameplay from the player's perspective with HUD visible, assume viewers give it **under about 10 seconds in the Discovery Queue** and watch without sound. A trailer is not stated as mandatory in what I read (search-only sources are silent too). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer
- **Tags:** at least 5 are required before launch; up to 20 matter; the top 5 should clearly describe the game, rarer tags carry more weight than generic ones, and player-applied tags blend with yours. Demos inherit the base game's tags. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/tags
- Description rules: no links to other sites, no imagery imitating Steam UI, no promotion of other Steam games. Publishing the store page sends all data to Valve's public servers even for unreleased apps. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page
- What the wave looks like on Steam today (data from our docs): about 100+ tidy-up releases since mid-August, most under $6 with under 50 reviews; the highest scorers are games with a distinct world (cat, wiki, witch, museum, cellar) at $3-8, and quality signal appears to come from theme and a human-made look more than object count. [fetched locally] Docs/competitive/15-refresh-2026-10-17.md
- Librarian's own page, for reference: 20 user tags starting Relaxing, Organizing, Collectathon, Artificial Intelligence, Singleplayer, Casual, Puzzle...; an "AI Generated Content Disclosure" section with the developer's text; one YouTube trailer. [fetched] https://store.steampowered.com/app/4197610/
- **What a cozy tidying game needs to stand out** [derived from the above plus local competitive docs]: a capsule that reads at 462x174 (logo-forward, warm lamp-lit library, no extra text); a trailer whose first 10 seconds show the satisfying place-a-book-and-it-lights moment from the player's eye with no audio dependence; screenshots of the real in-game view (reviews in our docs punish games whose items cannot be read in piles); a differentiator in the first line of the short description that is not an item count (count is "no longer a differentiator" per Docs/competitive/README.md): story wrapper (inherited library), real ordering rules, restoration. Tags: lead with the most specific (e.g. Organizing, Relaxing, Cozy, Mystery if true) and keep "Artificial Intelligence" off unless the game really features it (it appears on Librarian, likely player-applied because of its AI controversy; the tags page says players can apply tags). [derived]

### 2.4 SteamPipe (depots, builds, upload)

- SteamPipe splits files into roughly 1 MB chunks, compresses and encrypts them, and uploads only changed chunks on updates. Builds go to branches; you can roll back. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading
- Set up in Steamworks first: define at least one launch option (executable path, arguments, OS); on the Depots page name each depot and set its OS and language; publish; **add the depots to a package** (e.g. the Developer Comp package) or you will not own/see the build; give the upload a dedicated build account with "Edit App Metadata" and "Publish App Changes To Steam". Setting a build live on a released app needs a phone number or Steam Mobile App on that account. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading
- Scripts (VDF), from the SDK's `tools/ContentBuilder`: an app build script (`AppID`, `Desc`, `ContentRoot`, `BuildOutput`, `Preview`, `SetLive`, `Depots`) and a depot build script (`DepotID`, `FileMapping` with `LocalPath`/`DepotPath`/`Recursive`, `FileExclusion`). Naming convention `app_build_<AppID>.vdf` and `depot_build_<DepotID>.vdf`; `simple_app_build.vdf` is the starter. Upload command: `steamcmd.exe +login <account> <password> +run_app_build <script> +quit`. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading
- `SetLive` can push to a beta branch automatically; **the default branch must be set live by hand** in App Admin (partner.steamgames.com/apps/builds/<AppID>). For CI, log in once interactively and keep `config.vdf` so later runs need no password. `Preview` writes logs and a manifest and uploads nothing, which is a safe dry run. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading
- Common error: "Invalid content configuration" at launch means no build is live on the branch, the launch options are invalid, or the depots are not in a package you own. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/uploading
- Steam officially supports Windows 7 and above (page text; Unity 6.6 itself needs newer Windows, which is a Unity matter, not a Steam one). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/platforms
- Unity-specific build configuration [UNSOURCED GUESS, from memory of how Unity writes player builds; verify on a real build]: build Windows x86_64 to a clean folder; the folder will contain `BookBoy.exe`, `UnityPlayer.dll`, `BookBoy_Data/`, and (IL2CPP / Burst) extra `*_BurstDebugInformation_DoNotShip` and `*_BackUpThisFolder_ButDontShipItWithYourGame` folders that should be excluded with `FileExclusion`; `steam_appid.txt` must not be in the depot (Valve: "You should not ship this with your builds"). [steam_appid rule: fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/api

### 2.5 Steamworks SDK integration in Unity

- **Options.** (a) The official Valve SDK is a native C/C++ library (`steam_api64.dll` plus headers); Valve ships no Unity C# package in the pages I read, so Unity needs a wrapper. [derived from https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/api] (b) **Steamworks.NET**: MIT, a C# wrapper that "stays close to the original C++ API, so Valve's documentation largely applies", works with Unity, Windows 32/64, macOS, Linux; Unity install via UPM git URL `https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET.git?path=/com.rlabrecque.steamworks.net` (pin a tag), minimum Unity 2019.4; `SteamManager` (a MonoBehaviour you drop in the first scene) initialises and calls `RunCallbacks`. [fetched] https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET , https://steamworks.github.io/installation/ , https://steamworks.github.io/gettingstarted/ (c) **Facepunch.Steamworks**: MIT, Unity and IL2CPP marked as supported, a more C#-idiomatic API (`SteamClient.Init(appId)`), commits as recent as 2026-10-13. [fetched] https://github.com/Facepunch/Facepunch.Steamworks and its commits page.
- Version disagreement within Steamworks.NET's own pages: the README says it builds against **SDK 1.65**; the releases list I fetched showed 2025.164.x (SDK 1.64) as the newest entries, with no years shown on the page. Treat the exact current version as unknown until you open the Releases page yourself. [fetched, sources disagree] https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET and https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET/releases
- **Domain-reload relevance:** the Steamworks.NET 2025.162.1 release notes say it "Resets the Callback Dispatcher on Unity domain reload to stop error spam". BookBoy runs with Enter Play Mode domain reload disabled (CLAUDE.md rule 9; Docs/unity-practices/13). Keep Steam code behind a plain-C# interface and test Play Mode start/stop with it. [fetched + derived] https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET/releases
- **Minimum for a shipped game** (Valve's API page): call `SteamAPI_RestartAppIfNecessary(appId)` first, "optional but highly recommended"; if it returns true, quit the process "as soon as possible"; then `SteamAPI_Init` ("MUST be called and return successfully prior to accessing any of the Steamworks Interfaces"); call `SteamAPI_RunCallbacks` often (once per frame is typical, "at least once a second"); call `SteamAPI_Shutdown` at exit. `steam_appid.txt` next to the exe lets you run outside Steam in development and must not ship. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sdk/api
- Whether the `SteamManager` script in Steamworks.NET already contains the RestartAppIfNecessary call and Application.Quit: I believe yes with a placeholder AppId you must edit, but I could not confirm (the docs page for it returned 404; the Getting Started page does not cover it). UNSOURCED GUESS. Facepunch equivalent of RestartAppIfNecessary: not mentioned in its README; UNSOURCED GUESS that one exists.
- **Recommendation among the three:** Steamworks.NET, because Valve's own docs map one-to-one onto it (fetched README claim), it has a Unity-specific installer and `SteamManager`, and its changelog shows it has fixed domain-reload issues. Facepunch is a legitimate alternative. [derived]
- **Auto-Cloud for saves:** needs no code. In Steamworks set a per-user byte quota and file-count quota, then define Root Paths (Root, Subdirectory, Pattern, OS restriction, Recursive). Steam syncs when the app launches and exits. Unity's `Application.persistentDataPath` on Windows is `%userprofile%\AppData\LocalLow\<companyname>\<productname>`, which corresponds to the **WinAppDataLocalLow** root in Auto-Cloud; use a subdirectory like `<companyname>/<productname>/Saves` and pattern `*.json`. Changes take up to 10 minutes to reach clients; test with developer-only mode and Steam console command `testappcloudpaths <AppId>`; log at `Steam/logs/cloud_log.txt`. Smaller files and separate files for state that changes at different rates reduce re-uploads. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/cloud and https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Application-persistentDataPath.html
- Because the Windows path includes the company and product name, changing Player Settings company/product name after saves exist moves the folder (the Unity page does not say this outright; it only describes how the path is built). [derived] That means setting final names before the first public build; ProjectSettings changes need Kolby's approval under CLAUDE.md.
- **Leaderboards:** configured in Steamworks App Admin or via `FindOrCreateLeaderboard`; sort ascending (times) or descending (scores); display Numeric/Seconds/Milliseconds; up to 10,000 per title; one entry per player per board; score is an int32 plus up to 64 int32 of associated data; "Keep best" or "force update" upload; setting Writes to **Trusted** stops clients writing scores (only the Web API can). The page is silent on cheating beyond that and on whether an app must be released first. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/leaderboards . Per-zone boards fit the concept in Docs/13 (a run timer/score per zone).
- **Achievements (later):** configured in App Admin, unlocked with `SetAchievement` then `StoreStats`; capped at 100 until the app reaches a profile-features threshold; before release they do not show in Community, so the game needs its own in-game display; Valve recommends turning them off in a demo. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/achievements and the demos page above.

### 2.6 Steam Input and controller support (short note, later task)

- The **Steam Input API is not required**: "It's perfectly acceptable to ship conventional gamepad input as well as Steam Input." When Steam sends gamepad input it appears in the game as regular Xbox-controller input, so a game using Unity's Input System with Xbox bindings works through Steam. For full Steam Input support, set the Steam Input template and action manifest in Steamworks and ship a default configuration for every action set. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller/getting_started_for_devs
- I did not find the Full/Partial controller-support declaration rules; not read. UNSOURCED GUESS that the store page "Controller support" field is set in Edit Store Page > Basic Info.

### 2.7 Steam Deck Verified (brief)

- Four categories: Verified, Playable, Unsupported, Unknown. Verified needs: default controller configuration reaching all content with no in-game settings change; on-screen glyphs matching the active input device (Steam Input API recommended); text entry via Steamworks on-screen keyboard or built-in controller text entry; **30 fps at 1280x800**; no launcher or warning about unsupported hardware/Linux; smallest on-screen font **at least 9 px tall at 1280x800** (12 px recommended); resolution 1280x800 preferred or 1280x720. Games without a native Linux build run through Proton, and blocking Proton bugs mean Unsupported. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/compat
- Not needed to sell. Verification is not required for release in anything I read. DinoBones got Deck Verified on Oct 7 2026 per our docs. [fetched locally] Docs/competitive/15-refresh-2026-10-17.md
- The 16:9 PC target is compatible: Deck's 1280x800 is 16:10 (derived), so UI must tolerate 16:10 safe areas. See `17-ui-scaling-and-world-space-text.md` (CanvasScaler Match and minimum font findings overlap with the 9 px rule).

### 2.8 Steam's AI content disclosure (matters: book models and textures)

- Steamworks has a **Generative AI Content** section in the content survey, filled out **before** the store page and build can be reviewed. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey
- **Pre-generated:** AI-assisted content that ships with the game and that players consume (art, sound, narrative, localization) "must be described in detail"; Valve reviews it like any other content, including that it is legal, non-infringing and consistent with marketing. **Live-generated:** AI that runs during play, plus a description of guardrails against illegal content. **Exempt:** development efficiency tools ("Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus"). [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey
- Book **models and textures made with AI are shipped, player-visible, pre-generated content, so they must be disclosed and described.** Code assistants like Claude/Copilot for source code are efficiency tools and fall under the exemption. [derived]
- Some survey answers cannot be edited after approval without contacting Steam Support. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey
- The disclosure is shown on the store page (Librarian's page carries an "AI Generated Content Disclosure" section with the developer's text). [fetched] https://store.steampowered.com/app/4197610/ . The Steamworks page I read does not itself say how it is displayed to players.
- Valve rewrote the guidance on **2026-01-16**, narrowing disclosure to content that ships and removing back-end tools from the requirement; a Steam overlay button for players to report illegal AI content was added. These come from secondary reporting; I did not find Valve's own change note. [search-only] https://tbreak.com/steam-ai-disclosure-policy-update-developers/ , https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines
- **Market reaction is the larger risk than the rule.** From our competitive docs: AI mentions in negative reviews run 65% (Librarian), 55% (Mall), 28% (Supermarket Chaos) down to 4% (Shelve the Potions); the sharp new pattern is "disclosure added after I bought it" and "claimed none, found some". DinoBones' top negative review (~500 helpful votes) accused generated-looking wall art and a map despite a "no AI" claim; Black Friday Janitor drew accusations about bought asset packs; "Lesson: a 'no AI' claim is a liability unless every visible asset can back it." Folded Fables' "no gen AI with receipts" and Shelve the Potions ("zero AI influence" volunteered by a reviewer) are the positive counter-examples. [fetched locally] Docs/competitive/README.md and Docs/competitive/15-refresh-2026-10-17.md
- **Copyright:** the US Copyright Office's Part 2 report (2025-01-29) concludes prompts alone do not give authorship, wholly AI-generated output is not protected, and human selection/arrangement/modification can be; case by case. Practical effect: a purely AI-generated book model or texture may not be protectable by you, and may be copied freely by others. [search-only] https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/ , https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/ . Not legal advice; I did not research other countries.

### 2.9 Unity licensing for a Steam game

- **Runtime Fee: cancelled.** Unity's page says the cancellation is "effective immediately", decided **2024-09-12**, and that no new or existing games are or will be subject to the fee, including Unity 6. [fetched] https://unity.com/runtime-fee and https://unity.com/pricing-updates
- **Unity Personal** is available to customers with up to **$200,000 in revenue and funding** (prior 12 months; for small businesses it is aggregate gross revenue and funding). ### 2.11 Refunds and the 2-hour rule

- Policy: refund for any reason within **14 days** of purchase and **under 2 hours** of playtime; pre-release purchases can be refunded any time before release and the 14 days starts at release; refunds issue within a week. [fetched] https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/
- Refunds are deducted in the monthly report, not in the live Sales & Activations numbers. [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting
- Effect on a short game: a game that can be seen through in under 2 hours can be refunded in full after finishing it. Our competitive docs record short games in the wave: Tidy Up History about 3 hours, Paver one map about 90 minutes in co-op, Wand & Order "very short", Spellbound about 2 hours of waiting; reviewers asked for replay modes. [fetched locally] Docs/competitive/15-refresh-2026-10-17.md . I found no sourced refund-rate figure for this genre. UNSOURCED GUESS that the cheapest defence is making the first 2 hours obviously not the whole game (progression gates, multiple rooms) and giving a replay/free-play path.

### 2.12 Pricing norms and competitor data

Wishlisters are emailed on a discount only if it is at least 20%, lasts more than 8 hours and covers the lowest-priced package. ## 5. Implication for BookBoy

An ordered checklist. Items 1 to 3 cost little and start clocks; most later items depend on knowing what the game is.

1. **Decide the publishing identity first** (sole proprietorship in Kolby's legal name vs a company; do not use a DBA; the bank account holder name must match). Check with an accountant about keeping it separate from MTM Environmental, and read the Unity Editor Software Terms once with that identity in mind. No Valve rule forces an entity. (2.1, 2.9)
2. **Decide the AI policy now, before any asset is made, and write it down** in the repo (e.g. a provenance log: per asset, tool, prompt, human edits). Rule of thumb from the market: disclose precisely what is AI-made in the Steam survey and on the store page, keep the disclosure true for posters, UI art and key art too, and do not claim "no AI" unless every visible asset supports it. If hand-made or procedurally built book assets are viable for the hero props, the competitive data says that is the safer reputation. Also note AI-only output may not be copyrightable. (2.8)
3. **Sign up for Steamworks and pay the $100 fee early** (it starts the 21/30-day clock and the paperwork takes 15-30 days); then complete tax, bank and identity. This also gives you an App ID to test Steamworks.NET, Cloud and leaderboards against, instead of Spacewar 480. Fee is recouped after $1,000 revenue. (2.1)
4. **Create `Docs/` backlog items** for: Steam integration, store page assets, demo, content survey, pricing, release checklist (per CLAUDE.md the backlog is `Docs/10-backlog.md`).
5. **Before the first save-format release**, freeze Player Settings company and product names (the persistentDataPath depends on them), and put save data in a small number of small JSON files with atomic writes (write temp, then replace) and multiple slots; competitors were hit hard for save corruption and save-UI confusion. Needs Kolby's approval for ProjectSettings. (2.5; save-corruption evidence in Docs/competitive)
6. **Add Steamworks.NET behind a plain-C# interface** (`ISteamService` with a no-op implementation for editor and tests), initialise once with `RestartAppIfNecessary` then `Init`, run callbacks per frame, shut down at exit, and handle the "Steam not running" case by letting the game run offline without leaderboards. Test Play Mode start/stop since domain reload is disabled. Do not edit `Packages/manifest.json` without approval (the UPM git URL is a manifest change). (2.5)
7. **Auto-Cloud:** define WinAppDataLocalLow root, subdirectory `<company>/<product>/Saves`, pattern `*.json`; test with developer-only mode on two machines. (2.5)
8. **Leaderboards:** one board per zone, ascending for times or descending for score as the scoring rule decides; set Writes to Trusted only if you build a Web API server (otherwise accept client-written scores). Achievements later, using the same API; keep them off the demo. (2.5)
9. **Build a store page prototype early** (capsule, 5+ real in-game screenshots with readable book spines, a 30 to 60 second gameplay trailer opening on the place-a-book moment, tags, short description with a non-count hook). Valve suggests going public once art direction and core features are settled; there is no penalty for a long runway. (2.2, 2.3)
10. **Fill in the content survey including the AI section** before store-page review; keep answers consistent with the page. (2.8, 2.10)
11. **Make a demo as a separate App ID** (its own depot and build, own content survey, 5 demo screenshots if it has a separate page). Plan around Next Fest: Feb 22-Mar 1 2027 needs the demo build submitted about 5 weeks earlier (mid to late January) and a public Coming Soon page; Jun 14-21 2027 is the more realistic target for a game with saves, several zones and leaderboards. A game can join only one Next Fest, so use it when the demo is polished, not as a first test. Release after the fest; send the one demo wishlist email within 2 weeks of the demo going live and keep it 2 weeks away from other wishlist emails. (2.2)
12. **Set up SteamPipe:** dedicated build account, depot, launch option pointing at `BookBoy.exe`, package association, `app_build_<id>.vdf`, upload to a beta branch first, then set the default branch live by hand. Exclude `*_DoNotShip` and backup folders and `steam_appid.txt` (verify on a real build). (2.4)
13. **Pricing:** the comparable band is $4.99-$5.99 with a 10-20% launch discount; a multi-zone, narrative, saving game with leaderboards sits above the scale of those games, but our sample shows no strong-scoring sorter at $10 or more, so start the thinking at $7.99 to $9.99 (UNSOURCED GUESS) and let content hours decide. Do not plan a discount within 30 days of release. (2.12)
14. **Refund hedge:** make sure the first 2 hours cannot exhaust the game (several rooms, a progression path, free-play afterwards). (2.11)
15. **Deck and controller (later):** keep minimum on-screen text at or above 12 px at 1280x800 and the UI tolerant of 16:10 now, since it is cheap; defer Steam Input / Verified until after the PC release; gamepad via the Input System works through Steam as Xbox input. (2.6, 2.7)
16. **Submit order near the end:** store page for review first, then build; leave 7 business days ahead of the target date plus a spare week; keep the final 14 days free of date changes; avoid weekend release; release after a Next Fest rather than before; check the Winter/Spring/Summer sale eligibility (released 30 days before). Do a final pass on the AI disclosure text against the actual shipped assets. (2.2)
