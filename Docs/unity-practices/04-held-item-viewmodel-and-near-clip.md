# 04 - Held items in first person: viewmodel rendering, near clip, and the "shrink it" hack

Researched 2026-10-18. Web research plus read-only inspection of project scripts. Unity was not opened. Project: Unity 6000.6, URP (Render Graph era), Cinemachine 3 brain camera, Starter Assets FirstPersonController.

Tags: **[fetched]** = page body read; **[search-only]** = only a search-result summary seen; **[repo]** = read from files in this repo; **[derived]** = my arithmetic; **UNSOURCED GUESS** = could not source.

## 1. Question

How should held items be drawn in first person in URP: camera stacking, the Render Objects renderer feature, a separate viewmodel FOV? How do you stop a held object clipping through walls and shelves? What near clip plane value is sensible and what are the depth-precision trade-offs at small scales? Is shrinking the held object a sound hack, and what does it break (lighting, shadows, physics)?

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 What the project does now [repo]

- `CarryInventory` parents a picked-up book to `carryPoint`, turns its collider off and makes it kinematic, and draws it at `carryScale` (0.55 in Lamplit; 0.6 default) of its real size: "Carried books are shown at this fraction of their real size so they don't occlude as much of the view." The extra books in the fan are 0.82 of that. Files: Assets/_Project/Scripts/Interaction/CarryInventory.cs, Assets/_Project/Editor/LamplitBuilder.Hub.cs (line 196). Near clip is 0.2 and FOV 75 (project measurement note, Docs/competitive/scale-and-camera-comparison.md line 103). So the shrink is for occlusion and look, not (primarily) for clipping.

### 2.2 Option A - URP camera stacking (Base + Overlay)

- A stack is one Base camera plus overlay cameras; "A camera stack overrides the output of the Base Camera with the combined output of all the cameras in the camera stack." Each camera's Culling Mask decides what it draws; remove the held-item layer from the Base camera and keep only it on the overlay. [fetched] https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/cameras/urp/multiple/camera-stacking and https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/camera-stacking.html
- Overlay cameras keep their own Projection and Clipping Planes (Field of View and Near/Far are listed for the camera), and expose a **Clear Depth** option; they lack Stack and Output sections and several Rendering/Environment options (Anti-aliasing, Dithering, Stop NaNs, Opaque Texture, Background Type are Base-only). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/camera-component-reference.html
- Post-processing: URP "renders the post-processing effects only once, not repeatedly for each camera", so apply it on the last camera; mixing 2D and 3D renderers in one stack is unsupported; "be careful not to order the cameras in a way that causes excessive overdraw". [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/cameras/camera-stacking-concepts.html
- Cost: "rendering is faster if you remove layers that contain unneeded GameObjects" (culling masks default to everything). [fetched] 6000.6 camera-stacking page above. A search-result summary also says every active camera runs the full render loop, so a viewmodel stack costs one extra pass [search-only].
- Overlay cameras and stacking need the Universal Renderer; reports say overlay cameras render with the Forward path even when the base camera is Deferred. [search-only] https://discussions.unity.com/t/urp-deferred-camera-stack-not-possible/863620
- Cinemachine: "All Cinemachine does is move the camera around and drive the lens settings. There is nothing in it to prevent you from implementing overlay cameras." The thread's author still could not get the stack to appear and no fix is posted. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/cinemachine-camera-stacking/1597277 . My inference: the overlay camera has its own FOV, so you must copy or fix its FOV yourself (the Brain only writes lens to its own Camera). [derived/inference]
- Depth effects: with a second camera that clears depth, effects that read depth (AO, edge detection) are affected: "the second rendering will clear the depth buffer". [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/fps-gun-clippes-through-walls/12773 (2011, built-in pipeline, but the principle holds). A closed Unity issue says an overlay camera did not render depth correctly with custom depth shaders, fixed in URP 13. [search-only] https://issuetracker-mig.prd.it.unity3d.com/issues/urp-overlay-camera-in-camera-stack-does-not-render-depth-view-from-custom-shader

### 2.3 Option B - Render Objects renderer feature (same camera, own FOV)

- Unity's "Render Objects" feature has Overrides > Camera: "Overrides camera matrices and uses perspective projection." **Field Of View** "Defines the field of view when rendering objects, measured in degrees along the vertical axis" and is used instead of the Camera's; **Position Offset** shifts objects relative to the camera; **Restore** resets the camera matrices after the feature's passes. Overrides > Depth has Write Depth and Depth Test; Set As Input Attachment is a mobile bandwidth option. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/renderer-features/renderer-feature-render-objects.html and (Unity 6000.0) https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/renderer-features/renderer-feature-render-objects.html . The page does not mention first-person use.
- Unity's own sample, UniversalRenderingExamples, has a "First Person Objects" demo (`_CompletedDemos/FPSCameraCompleted/FPSCameraDemo.unity`) that renders first-person-layer objects "with a FOV(Field Of View) that differs from the game scene rendering FOV" because "the FOV needed for the experience is too wide for the objects held in hand", with "Depth settings to avoid rendering inside close objects like walls" and stencil settings for transparencies. It gives no numbers. [fetched] https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/UniversalRenderingExamples
- Known side effect: a Unity issue tracker entry titled "Ambient occlusion appears incorrect when character is rendered with a fixed FOV using Render Objects"; SSAO relies on the depth prepass depth, and suggested fixes were to disable SSAO or add a Render Objects pass that writes depth before the prepass. Title confirmed [fetched]; the details come from a search summary [search-only] https://issuetracker-mig.prd.it.unity3d.com/issues/ambient-occlusion-appears-incorrect-when-character-is-rendered-with-a-fixed-fov-using-render-objects (page returned 502 for the body).
- Unity 6 note: Render Graph is the default URP path and custom passes must be rewritten with the Render Graph API; Render Objects is a built-in feature, so you do not need to write a pass. [search-only] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/upgrade-guide-unity-6-1.html . I could not open the 6000.6 version of the Render Objects page (404); the 6000.0 and current-manual pages agree on the Camera overrides.
- Compared with a second camera, a same-camera feature keeps the object in the normal lighting and shadow-receiving passes (my inference; the HDRP forum thread below shows custom passes that render elsewhere in the frame lose SSS and shadows).
- HDRP counter-example (not our pipeline): custom-pass first-person rendering caused black SSS materials, missing shadows and cascade flicker, because such passes "render at one point in the pipeline, so they aren't included in all the passes that regular objects go through." [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/override-camera-fov-for-arms-gun-in-first-person-mode-hdrp/1595075

### 2.4 Option C - shader/vertex FOV trick and Option D - shrinking the object

- Epic ships this as an engine feature in UE 5.5: first-person primitives get their own horizontal FOV and a **First Person Scale** that "shrink[s] them closer towards the camera" so they fit in front of the near plane; geometry is then "very small and slightly skewed" and correct only from the camera's viewpoint. Caveats: first-person primitives do not cast shadows into the scene or appear in ray-traced reflections; self-shadowing is screen-space and limited to on-screen casters; scale too small makes the primitive vanish because it "intersects the near clipping plane"; you may need to adjust the global near clip plane. [fetched] https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/first-person-rendering . Key point: Epic scales **toward the camera** (preserving the projected image), not about the object's own pivot.
- Unity forum: one pipeline "scaled the fps arms and weapons down so they are fairly small" so they don't intersect world geometry, kept correct light and shadows, and applied a material with a FOV setting of 40 to render them at the correct size (scaling them up again in the vertex stage) without a second camera. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/threads/fps-arms.637918/ (2019, karstenv, described as how another project does it)
- Same hack with a naive transform scale: a forum reply on an HDRP weapon-clipping thread suggests "reducing the scale of all first-person models, which is simpler"; others report side effects such as blur and SSAO see-through issues for the render-on-top methods. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/hdrp-weapon-clipping-solved/859261
- HDRP thread (same custom-FOV vertex approach): "self-shadowing broke, because the visible mesh is scaled while shadow geometry must stay unscaled." [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/override-camera-fov-for-arms-gun-in-first-person-mode-hdrp/1595075
- A search summary of a Godot thread says a scaled-down weapon puts shadows in wrong places because the world is relatively huge versus the weapon, and a separate viewport cannot get environment lighting. [search-only; thread body not loaded] https://godotforums.org/d/28766-better-way-to-handle-first-person-weapon-clipping . Treat as anecdote.
- Plain transform scaling does not keep the screen size: halving a book at the same distance halves its apparent size; only scaling toward the camera (Epic) or compensating with a smaller FOV in a separate pass preserves the look. [derived]
- Physics: scaling a Rigidbody-controlled object changes how it behaves (Unity does not adjust mass or drag for you; a community answer, search-only, see `14-physics-small-objects-and-settings.md`). In this project the held book is kinematic with its collider disabled (2.1), so the scale does not interact with physics; the risk is on release, where `Release` restores `originalScale`. The existing code stores the original scale to avoid compounding. [repo]

### 2.5 Other no-render-change techniques

- Pull the held item back (or lower it) when a wall is near, via a short raycast/spherecast from the camera to the carry point: described as what "many games" do by a Steam developer comment [search-only] https://steamcommunity.com/app/731590/discussions/0/1694919808739292358 ; a UFPS forum thread says UFPS had a built-in "pushing the weapon back from colliders" behavior that stopped working when they switched rendering path. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/ufps-lwrp-weapon-camera-issue/766711
- Physical option: let walls push a real held rigidbody aside via a joint; heavier to build and not needed for a kinematic book. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/holding-objects-how-to-deal-with-object-clipping/87224
- The CharacterController radius already bounds how near the camera can get to a wall: radius 0.5 + skin 0.02 means about 0.52 m (see `03-eye-height-and-character-controller.md`). Reducing the radius makes held-item clipping more likely. [derived]

### 2.6 Near clip plane and depth precision

- Unity: Near is "The closest point relative to the camera that drawing will occur"; the camera "cannot see geometry that is closer than this distance." No numeric guidance in the manual. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-Camera.html and https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Camera-nearClipPlane.html
- Cinemachine 3 tooltips: raising Near "will stop the game from drawing things near the camera"; a larger value "will also increase your shadow resolution"; keep Far "as low as possible without cutting off desired distant objects." [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.LensSettings.html
- Depth buffer: the near plane "has such a profound effect on depth precision"; pushing Far to infinity matters little. In a simulation with near 0.1 and far 10K, reversed-Z with a float32 buffer left 0% depth comparisons indistinguishable versus 45% for the standard mapping; reversed-Z with int24 was worse (76% indistinguishable) than float32. His conclusion: use a floating-point depth buffer with reversed-Z. [fetched] https://www.reedbeta.com/blog/depth-precision-visualized/
- Unity uses reversed Z on DirectX, Metal and Vulkan ("1.0 at the near plane to 0.0 at the far plane"); OpenGL ES and WebGL use the traditional direction. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/SL-PlatformDifferences.html . A legacy-manual summary says reversed Z with a float depth buffer "massively increases the depth buffer precision ... especially with small near planes and large far planes". [search-only, 5.4 manual] https://docs.unity.cn/540/Documentation/Manual/SL-PlatformDifferences.html
- What depth format URP allocates on Windows (24-bit integer vs 32-bit float) I could not source. UNSOURCED GUESS: D24S8 or D32S8 depending on setting; this matters because Reed shows reversed-Z helps int24 less than float32.
- Unity forum (2015/2016): "There's a fixed amount of depth buffer precision, so the larger the ratio, the more that precision is spread out"; "The default that Unity uses of .3 near and 1000 far works well"; the old 0.1 default "was causing problems in some cases". [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/recommended-minimum-near-clipping-plane-of-cameras/594073 (built-in pipeline, pre-reversed-Z era; likely less severe on DX11/12 now, UNSOURCED GUESS)
- A Unity issue tracker title: "reducing cameras near clipping plane reduces its effective far clipping plane". [search-only title] https://issuetracker.unity3d.com/issues/reducing-cameras-near-clipping-plane-reduces-its-effective-far-clipping-plane
- Old forum separate-camera recipe: viewmodel camera child of the main camera, near 0.01, far 5, Depth Only clear, depth above main. [fetched] https://discussions.unity.com/t/fps-gun-clippes-through-walls/12773 (2011, built-in).
- The project's numbers: near 0.2 / far (unknown, Starter prefab 500) gives far/near = 2,500 [derived; project far value not checked]. The sample Starter Assets prefab ships near 0.2, far 500. [repo]

## 4. Confidence and unknowns

- **High**: how URP camera stacking and Render Objects work and which settings exist (read from Unity docs); that Epic and Unity's own sample use a separate viewmodel FOV; that near plane dominates depth precision (Reed).
- **Medium**: that a same-camera Render Objects pass keeps scene lighting/shadow receiving while a second camera may not (inference plus a negative HDRP example); SSAO side effects (summary of an issue tracker entry whose body I could not open).
- **Low**: absolute near-plane recommendations for Unity 6 on Windows (the forum guidance predates reversed Z and I could not source the URP depth format); exact behavior of Cinemachine plus overlay cameras (one thread, no resolution).
- Unknown here: the hold distance of `carryPoint`, whether the project uses SSAO, depth/opaque texture or a Deferred path, and the project's far clip. These decide which option bites.
- Unity 6000.6 specifics: the 6000.6 camera-stacking page exists and has no listed changes; the 6000.6 Render Objects page 404'd. Guardrail: CLAUDE.md requires explicit approval before touching layers, tags or render-pipeline settings; Options A and B both need a layer and a renderer change.

## 5. Implication for BookBoy

- **Current setting (shrink to 0.55, collider off, kinematic) is fine and the smallest thing that works.** Physics is not involved while held, and the code restores the real scale on release. Its limits: it changes apparent size, and a small shrink does not prevent clipping, but the 0.52 m capsule standoff probably already does.
- **Biggest hidden coupling:** if the capsule radius is reduced (file 03), the held books can start intersecting shelves and walls. Test this first; do not shrink the capsule and the carry offset together without a clipping check.
- **If clipping shows up, smallest change first:**
  1. **Proximity pull-back, no render change:** a short spherecast from the camera along the carry direction; if it hits, move `carryPoint` toward the camera by the overlap (and/or lower the book). No layer/pipeline change, no approval needed beyond a normal slice. Sourced as common practice, not as a Unity recommendation.
  2. **Render Objects feature on a "Held" layer** with its own vertical FOV (say a tighter value than the world) and depth settings, per Unity's UniversalRenderingExamples demo. Needs a new layer and a renderer-asset change, so it requires explicit approval and a stated plan under CLAUDE.md. Retest SSAO if it is enabled.
  3. **Camera stack overlay** only if you also need a different near/far for the held layer; it costs another pass, needs the Cinemachine FOV mirrored by script, and the Cinemachine thread did not resolve.
- **Near clip:** 0.2 is fine for now (the capsule keeps walls farther than that). If held books are ever nearer than 0.2 m to the eye, lower to about 0.1 first and look for z-fighting; going to 0.01 is only sensible for a dedicated viewmodel camera with a far plane of a few metres. Keep Far as low as the longest sightline (hub is 24.8 m long, so far 50 to 100 would do) to buy back ratio. These are UNSOURCED GUESSES on numbers; the direction (raise Near or lower Far to gain precision) is sourced.
- **Do not scale a held item with a plain transform to fix clipping**; that shrinks its on-screen size and, if you compensate with distance, it just moves the problem. If the shrink-toward-the-camera approach is wanted later, do it as Epic does (scale the position toward the camera, or compensate with the viewmodel FOV), not about the book's own pivot.

## 6. Addendum 2026-10-19: the held stack (after Kolby's play test)

- **Problem:** the stack was anchored to the body (yaw only) at 1.10 m. Looking at the middle or top shelf it was out of view, and near a bookcase the pull-back pushed it toward the eye, out of view as well.
- **What the reference games do** (`Docs/competitive/scale-and-camera-comparison.md`, screenshots `librarian/09`, `10`, `11`, `shelves-and-sorcery/01`): Librarian's stack moves with the view: level or looking up only its near corner is in the lower right; looking down it rises into the right third. Shelves and Sorcery draws one huge book fixed at the right. Both keep the stack in the lower right at every pitch; neither shows it vanishing when a shelf is close.
- **Decision (own reasoning from those pictures plus this file's section 2.4, no new web source):** the stack follows the camera pitch by a factor (0.7 looking down, 0.95 looking up) instead of being fixed to the body, at an offset of (0.40, -0.27, 0.52) from the eye (about 27 degrees below the view centre, so at a level gaze about half of the top book is in frame). Near a bookcase it shrinks toward the eye (position and size scaled together, minimum 0.4, so the picture on screen stays the same; this is Epic's first-person scale in section 2.4), found with a box cast of the stack's cross-section (a thin ray slipped into the open bay of the case and missed the side panels and shelf boards). The Cinemachine lens near plane is lowered from 0.2 to 0.1 on the scene's camera (section 5's first step) so the shrunk stack is not cut. No layer, renderer feature or project setting was touched.
- **Unknowns:** how it feels in motion; whether 0.1 shows z-fighting anywhere (not seen in renders).
