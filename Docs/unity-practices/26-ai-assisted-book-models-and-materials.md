## 1. Question

Today every item is a Unity Cube primitive, scaled non-uniformly, with a letter (and optionally a digit) as child glyph objects on the spine. Kolby wants real book shapes and nicer cover materials/maps, using AI where it helps. Specifically:

- (a) What does a good game-ready low-poly book mesh look like, and can it be produced with no image or 3D AI (C# Mesh API, ProBuilder, or a headless Blender Python script)?
- (b) What can AI texture/material tools do for covers, what are their limits, how do we keep 100+ items consistent, and what are the texture/material/draw-call consequences in URP?
- (c) Are AI text-to-3D generators worth using for a simple book?
- (d) What are Unity's own generative tools and do they apply?
- (e) Steam disclosure, copyright status, tool licences, and what to record per asset.
- (f) A recommended pipeline and a tiny first experiment with screenshot-checkable acceptance criteria.

### 2.0 What the project does today (read from source, not web)

- Items are `GameObject.CreatePrimitive(PrimitiveType.Cube)` with `localScale = size`, `sharedMaterial = Mat_BookSpine`, a trigger `BoxCollider`, and a `Book` component. [fetched: `Assets/_Project/Editor/LamplitBuilder.Common.cs` lines 389-416]
- **Colour comes from a MaterialPropertyBlock, not from one material per colour.** `Book.SetColor` writes `_BaseColor` (and `_Color`) into a `MaterialPropertyBlock`, so every book shares the single `Mat_BookSpine`. [fetched: `Assets/_Project/Scripts/Books/Book.cs` lines 13-14, 93-106, 400-413; `Mat_BookSpine` is the only book material found by grep]. Files 09/10/11 assumed "one material per colour"; the code does not do that. Unity's docs say MaterialPropertyBlock is incompatible with the SRP Batcher and "will likely result in a drop in performance" in URP (file 10, https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/MaterialPropertyBlock.html). So books are probably already outside the SRP Batcher today. **[derived; to be confirmed with the Frame Debugger "Batch cause" row, which I did not run]**
- Seating: the slot places the book at `slot.position + up * (scale.y * 0.5) + forward * (scale.z * 0.5)` and rotates it with the slot, so the **pivot is the geometric centre** and the spine/"front" face is the slot's front line. [fetched: `Assets/_Project/Scripts/Books/ShelfSlot.cs` lines 41-50]. A real mesh with a different pivot, or a different axis convention, changes this maths.
- Backlog D-005 (2026-10-17) says "No generative AI for art or text"; T-004 (2026-10-18) asks for AI-assisted book models/covers. **These conflict; the newer item reads as the current intent, but the owner should confirm D-005 is superseded for textures/models** (D-005 also reads as scoped to a commissioning plan, so this research keeps non-AI routes first). [fetched: `Docs/10-backlog.md` lines 264-267, 300-302]
- Camera numbers used below: vertical FOV 75 degrees; a 0.38 m book at 1.5 m is about 17% of screen height (file 02, `Docs/unity-practices/README.md`). T-002 may change FOV and scale; the formulas in section 3 let you redo the sums.

### 2.1 (a) Good low-poly book mesh, and making it without AI

What a game-ready book looks like (mostly design knowledge; sources are weak):

- Marketplace listings give very different budgets: roughly 570-950 triangles for stylised books, about 612 for a small antique book, 5,000-17,000 for detailed hardcovers with the pages on a separate material. [search-only] https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/interior/office/books-stylized , https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/furniture/tableware/old-books-and-papers
- One vendor tutorial (Tripo, an AI-3D company, so not neutral) suggests a three-tier plan: a high-poly bake source, a mid-poly game model (about 1k-4k triangles) and a sub-500-triangle distant LOD, starting from a cube for the covers plus thin planes for pages. It is one author's opinion. [search-only] https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/how-to-make-a-book-3d-model (direct fetch returned HTTP 403)
- Our case is far below those numbers because it is a shelf prop seen at 0.5-1.5 m on a flat-colour look. A construction that needs only a few dozen to a couple of hundred triangles is **[derived]** below; no source gives a budget for this exact use. Everything about the pivot, UV split and rounded spine below is **design recommendation, UNSOURCED GUESS** as to what is optimal.

Generating it without any image/3D AI:

- **C# Mesh API (no extra software).** A `Mesh` is built from `vertices`, `triangles`, `uv`, `normals`; call `Clear()` before refilling a mesh; `RecalculateNormals()` and `RecalculateBounds()` exist; the Set* methods validate indices. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Mesh.html . If a normal map is used, call `RecalculateTangents()`; it needs normals and UV0, and if either is missing every tangent becomes (1,0,0,1) (wrong normal mapping). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Mesh.RecalculateTangents.html . `AssetDatabase.CreateAsset(asset, path)` saves it as a native asset and "overwrites" an existing asset at that path; the page does not say whether the GUID is kept. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/AssetDatabase.CreateAsset.html . CLAUDE.md rule 14 (never rebuild a referenced asset by delete+copy; it mints a new GUID) argues for loading the existing mesh asset and refilling it (`Clear()` + assign), not re-creating it. **UNSOURCED GUESS** whether CreateAsset preserves the GUID; verify by diffing the `.meta`.
- **ProBuilder.** Package for building/editing/texturing geometry, with UV editing, vertex colours, parametric shapes and a scripting API; the page lists prototyping, collision meshes and level design as uses and mentions a model export feature; it does not say whether the API works at runtime, and does not name OBJ/FBX. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.probuilder@6.0/manual/index.html . CLAUDE.md forbids changing `Packages/manifest.json` without approval, and ProBuilder is not needed for a parametric book, so I would not add it. **UNSOURCED GUESS**: ProBuilder is a poor fit for a parametric, scriptable, regeneratable book compared with ~100 lines of Mesh code.
- **Blender Python (bpy), headless.** Blender runs without a UI with `-b/--background`; `-P/--python <file>` runs a script; `--python-exit-code <0-255>` sets the exit code if the script raises (command line only); `--factory-startup` skips the user's startup file; arguments run in the order given; `--` ends Blender's option parsing and everything after goes to the script in `sys.argv`. [fetched] https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/advanced/command_line/arguments.html . Command shape (derived from those flags): `blender --background --factory-startup --python make_book.py --python-exit-code 1 -- <args>`.
  - **Blender is not on this PC's PATH, and `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation` does not exist** (I checked; it may be installed somewhere else, e.g. Steam or a portable copy). Using it means installing it. [fetched via local shell]
  - Licence: Blender is GPL, but "What you create with Blender is your sole property" including .blend and data files. The page does not address script output specifically. [fetched] https://www.blender.org/about/license/
  - FBX export operator `bpy.ops.export_scene.fbx`: `apply_scale_options` default `FBX_SCALE_NONE` (other values `FBX_SCALE_UNITS`, `FBX_SCALE_CUSTOM`, `FBX_SCALE_ALL`); `axis_forward` default `'-Z'`; `axis_up` default `'Y'`; `bake_space_transform` default `False` and marked experimental ("known to be broken with armatures/animations"); `use_mesh_modifiers` default `True`; `use_tspace` default `False`; `mesh_smooth_type` default `'OFF'`; `global_scale` default 1.0. [fetched] https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.ops.export_scene.html . Defaults that matter for Unity: tangents are off unless asked, smoothing is off (so set `mesh_smooth_type` explicitly, or have Unity calculate normals), and scale/axis must be verified by measuring the imported bounds. **UNSOURCED GUESS** on the best combination of axis/scale options; test by importing and reading `bounds.size`.
  - Blender's Python docs pages for `bmesh.ops` and baking are very large and I only got navigation text from the fetch; I could not verify specific `bmesh.ops` signatures or headless bake behaviour. **Unverified.**
- **Unity import of the result.** Unity says "Unity's primary support for model files is the FBX format"; native `.blend` files work only if Blender is installed on the same machine (Unity calls its FBX exporter), and the page recommends exporting FBX into `Assets` "in most cases". [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/ImportingModelFiles.html . Model import: Scale Factor/Convert Units, Normals (Import/Calculate/None), Tangents (Calculate Mikktspace), Read/Write disabled by default (keep off), Optimize Mesh, Generate Lightmap UVs (not needed for dynamic items). Unity expects 1 unit = 1 m. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/FBXImporter-Model.html
- **glTF is not the easy path.** Unity's glTFast package imports glTF in the Editor and at runtime and works with URP, but "Default Lit (URP/HDRP) ... materials are not yet supported for editor import" (6.0 page). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cloud.gltfast@6.0/manual/index.html . It is also a new package (manifest change). Use FBX.
- **One mesh, hundreds of instances.** Many `MeshRenderer`s referencing the same `Mesh` asset is the normal case, and the GPU Resident Drawer only helps when objects share mesh and material (file 10). A real book needs **a mesh per size class**, not per book, if you stop relying on non-uniform scale (see section 5).

### 2.2 (b) AI texture and material generation for covers

What the tools can do (all vendor/secondary sources unless stated):

- **Text/Image to tileable texture, and texture to PBR material.** Adobe Substance 3D Sampler 4.4 has Text to Texture (tileable results, several variations), Image to Texture (always square and tileable; the reference does not need to be tileable) and Image to Material (derives normal, height, roughness). Features were beta and limited to Adobe subscribers in May 2024; assets get Content Credentials metadata marking them AI-generated. [search-only, 2024] https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/05/adobe-releases-substance-3d-sampler-4-4 . I could not open Adobe's own help page (HTTP 403), and found nothing on current availability or on commercial terms.
- **Unity's own generators** list a Texture2D Generator and a Material Generator (PBR materials). See 2.4.
- **Normal/height/smoothness from a diffuse image without any AI:** Materialize (free, open source, GPL v3 per one source; made in Unity; Windows) creates Height, Metalness and Smoothness from a Diffuse, and converts Height to Normal and back. [search-only, 2018-2019 sources] https://gamefromscratch.com/materialize-free-texture-map-generation-tool-released/ . A generated normal from a flat cloth image is a heuristic, not a measurement. **UNSOURCED GUESS**: quality is fine for a subtle cloth weave and poor for embossed lettering.
- **Free non-AI sources of cloth/leather/paper textures:** ambientCG (CC0 1.0; commercial use allowed; no credit required; the licence page says nothing about AI) [fetched] https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/ , and Poly Haven (CC0; any purpose; no attribution required; redistribution allowed) [fetched] https://polyhaven.com/license . These are PBR sets (colour, normal, roughness) already tileable. Neither needs Steam AI disclosure.
- **Procedural textures (zero licence question):** a C# editor script or a Blender procedural material can generate a cloth weave, leather grain, border lines, and stamped patterns; Blender can bake them, but I could not verify the bake docs (page fetch returned only navigation). **UNSOURCED GUESS**: noise and weave are easy to generate in a few dozen lines, and it makes 100+ covers consistent by construction.

Limits and reliability:

- **Text on covers/spines.** Short, large text is now often readable, small text still fails. One roundup says small spine lettering is harder than signage and "still needs manual checking or an editable text layer"; a vendor-adjacent article claims GPT-Image-2 (April 2026) reaches near-perfect text; a side-by-side said smaller text drifted or was invented in some models. [search-only, vendor/affiliate blogs] https://www.capcut.com/create/ai-image-text-rendering-works-in-2026 , https://datanorth.ai/news/openai-launches-gpt-image-2-with-99-text-accuracy . Verdict: sources disagree on how good it is now, and none is independent. **Keep letters and numbers drawn by the game** (the project already does, as glyph child objects). Also an AI-painted letter would be the wrong letter for gameplay, since the letter is the sorting key.
- **Tileability and seams:** only some tools (Substance Text/Image to Texture) explicitly promise tileable output. Midjourney's docs page on style reference was not readable (HTTP 403), so I cannot cite its tile option. Seam check by screenshot is required for any plain image-model output. **UNSOURCED GUESS**: generic image models do not guarantee tiling.
- **Consistency across 100+ items.** Scenario (a Unity partner) markets custom-trained models for "consistent on-brand output" (marketing claim only). [search-only] https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/frequently-asked-questions-faq . I found no independent evidence of how consistent any tool is across 100 outputs. The robust way to get consistency is to **limit AI to making a handful of neutral base patterns** and apply per-item variety (colour, accent, spine letter) in code. That is a design judgement (**UNSOURCED GUESS**).

URP Lit setup for albedo + normal + smoothness:

- Lit inputs: Base Map (colour/texture; alpha only for transparency/clipping unless Smoothness Source is Albedo Alpha), Metallic Map (Metallic workflow), Smoothness (0-1; Source = Metallic Alpha by default, or Albedo Alpha; "Unity multiplies the texture values by the Smoothness value"), Normal Map (with intensity), Occlusion Map. A single RGBA texture can pack metallic, smoothness and occlusion. Workflow Mode affects batching. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/urp/lit-shader.html
- File 09 already recommends Smoothness 0, Metallic 0, Specular Highlights and Environment Reflections off for the flat look. A cloth cover wants the **normal map only for a faint weave**, and with low smoothness the specular term is tiny anyway (file 09: about 2% of diffuse at smoothness 0.2, derived). Therefore on the planned look a normal map is optional. **[derived]**
- Base Map is multiplied by the material colour (standard Lit behaviour; I did not fetch a page that states it, but `Book.cs` already relies on `_BaseColor` tinting). **UNSOURCED GUESS** that `_BaseColor` × Base Map is a straight multiply; confirm by screenshot.

Atlas vs texture array vs one material per colour (draw-call implications):

- Per file 10: the SRP Batcher batches by **shader variant, not material count**; "You can still use as many different materials with the same shader as you want"; MaterialPropertyBlock makes a renderer incompatible; GPU instancing needs the same mesh and material; static batching is "Disable" in URP. [fetched in file 10] https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/SRPBatcher.html , https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/optimizing-draw-calls-choose-method.html
- Texture arrays: a 2D texture array is "an array of 2D textures with the same size, format and flags"; the page says nothing about draw calls. [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-Texture2DArray.html . Sampling needs a custom Shader Graph (the Shader Graph array node page returned 404, so unverified). Picking the slice per book without a MaterialPropertyBlock needs the index baked into mesh data (**UNSOURCED GUESS**), which means one mesh per distinct cover.
- Atlas: a single big texture with each cover in a cell; same problem for choosing the cell per book (UV offset must live in mesh data, or in a per-book material, or in a MaterialPropertyBlock). **[derived]**
- Hence for this project: **materials are cheap, picking the texture per book is the hard part.** The simplest answer is a material per (cover pattern, colour) pair or per cover design, all on the same shader; that stays SRP-Batcher-compatible (file 10) but the number of materials grows multiplicatively (see section 3).
- **Tint problem to design around:** with a multiply tint (and the current MPB colour), the page block, if it is part of the same material, would also be tinted by the category colour. Options: (i) two submeshes/material slots (cover material tinted, paper material not) at the cost of a second draw per book, using the `SetPropertyBlock(properties, materialIndex)` overload so only the cover slot gets the colour [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/Renderer.SetPropertyBlock.html (the page says nothing about the SRP Batcher); (ii) bake the category colour into per-colour cover textures (no tint at runtime); (iii) a small Shader Graph that uses a vertex-colour mask (cover vs pages) and a colour property (SRP-Batcher-compatible per file 10 if properties live in `UnityPerMaterial`; custom shader work and a hard dependency on Shader Graph vertex colour behaviour that I did not verify).

Texture compression and mipmaps:

- Desktop formats from Unity's reference: BC7 (RGB/RGBA, high quality, 8 bpp, 1 MB for 1024x1024), BC5 (RG, high, 8 bpp), BC4 (R, high, 4 bpp), DXT1/BC1 (RGB, medium, 4 bpp, 0.5 MB at 1024). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/texture-formats-reference.html . The "choose format by platform" page recommends DXT1 for RGB, BC7 or DXT5 for RGBA (BC7 higher quality but slower to compress). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/texture-choose-format-by-platform.html . The pages do not name a normal map format; BC5 is my inference from the channel layout, not a Unity statement. **[derived]**
- Mipmaps "increase the size of a texture by 33%, both on disk and in memory", reduce artefacts and speed up distant sampling; skip them only for textures always shown at full resolution (UI). [fetched] https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/texture-mipmaps-introduction.html . Covers viewed at 0.5-1.5 m need mipmaps.
- Anisotropic filtering: Aniso Level 0-16, sharper at shallow angles, resource-intensive, and Unity suggests it for ground/floor textures. [search-only, Unity doc snippets] https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Texture-anisoLevel.html . Books on a shelf are viewed at oblique angles (spine faces seen from the side, covers when held), so a low level (2) is a candidate; **UNSOURCED GUESS**, test by screenshot.

### 2.3 (c) AI text-to-3D mesh generators

- **Quality and topology.** A vendor guide (checked by the author in September 2026) says raw output has "no edge loops, no sensible pole placement", defaults of "20k to 300k triangles per asset" (millions in high-effort modes), lighting baked into the albedo ("shadows painted into the texture"), and uneven texel density from poor auto-unwrapping; it says AI 3D works best today for static props, dressing and stylised items. [search-only; vendor guide] https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators . A second search summary reports a Tripo example of 900,248 faces decimated to 7,176, Meshy options of 5,000-100,000 faces with a low-poly mode and remesh, and Rodin as having the cleanest quads; the sources disagree on which tool has the best topology and none was an independent benchmark. [search-only] https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-3d-modeling-tools
- **Why a book is a poor fit** (derived): a book needs a hard-edged box with a slight round and a clean UV layout in which the cover texture is unwrapped as a flat rectangle. Generator output gives dense triangulated shells with arbitrary UV islands, which defeats our "one tiny shared mesh with a clean cover UV" requirement. **UNSOURCED GUESS** that a generated book would also tint and shade inconsistently between items, since each result differs.
- **Licences (all change; re-read before relying):**
  - Meshy: Free-plan output is CC BY 4.0 (commercial use only with attribution), Pro/Enterprise get a private licence with full commercial use and "you own the output"; sources disagree on free credits (100 vs 200) and per-generation costs; Text-to-3D costs 20 credits on Meshy 6. [fetched] https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing , plus [search-only] https://intercom.help/meshy/en/articles/16102098-can-i-use-meshy-assets-commercially (the help article returned 404 when fetched directly). Export formats include GLB, FBX, OBJ. [fetched] https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/platform/export-formats
  - Tripo: the vendor guide says the free tier is non-commercial. [search-only] https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators
  - Hunyuan3D 2.1 (open model): the licence "DOES NOT APPLY IN THE EUROPEAN UNION, UNITED KINGDOM AND SOUTH KOREA", Territory excludes those, there is a >1M monthly-active-user clause, and Section 5(b) bans using Output to improve other AI models. A Steam game sold into the EU/UK would be selling into an unlicensed territory under this licence. [fetched] https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE . This matters because Unity's 3D generator reportedly defaults to a Hunyuan 3D model (see 2.4); Unity's own terms for hosted use are a separate contract and I could not verify how they interact.
  - Stability AI Community License: free commercial use for organisations under USD 1M annual revenue; "you own outputs"; enterprise licence above that; revocable on violation. [fetched] https://stability.ai/license
- Triangle counts for a book from a generator would need a decimation pass and a UV fix. That is more work than the 100-line procedural version.

### 2.5 (e) Legal and platform

Steam:
- Valve's Content Survey separates **Pre-Generated** content (AI-made during development, ships with the game and is consumed by players) from **Live-Generated** content (created while the game runs; also needs guardrail descriptions). Pre-generated is judged "the same way we evaluate all non-AI content" for illegal/infringing content and marketing consistency. Efficiency tools used only in development "fall outside the focus". [fetched] https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey
- January 2026 rewrite, as reported: AI-powered tools (e.g. code helpers) need no disclosure; disclosure is required for AI used to generate content in the game, on the store page or in marketing, and for content generated during gameplay; the store page shows a section "AI Generated Content Disclosure"; developers describe tools in a text box. [fetched] https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use , https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam . VGC also calls the disclosures "voluntary" while saying Steam requires them; unreconciled. I could not retrieve Valve's verbatim current form wording.
- So: AI-generated cover textures or AI-generated meshes that ship = pre-generated content = **disclose**. CC0 textures, hand-made or procedurally coded meshes and textures = nothing to disclose. A mesh produced by a script that an AI coding assistant wrote is not clearly covered either way (Valve names "code helpers" as exempt, but the shipped mesh is content). **UNSOURCED GUESS**: treat it as not AI-generated content (the program is deterministic), and say so in the text box if you want to be conservative.

Copyright (US):
- USCO Part 2 (January 2025): prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make the user the author of the output, even with iterative prompting; protection can attach to the human-authored selection/coordination/arrangement and to creative human modifications; human-authored inputs perceptible in the output are protectable; applicants should disclaim AI-generated parts when registering; no new sui generis right is recommended. [fetched, via the Copyright Alliance summary and the Office's press page; the PDF fetch returned the wrong document so I did not read the report itself] https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/ , https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html
- Consequences (derived): raw AI covers are probably not copyrightable by us, so anyone could reuse them; heavy human editing, a human-designed arrangement, and procedural/hand-made assets are. Other jurisdictions I did not research (**UNSOURCED**).

- **Texel density target:** one texel per screen pixel at the closest typical view. A 0.38 m cover at 512 px is 1,347 texels/m, about 1:1 with 1080p at 0.5 m; at 1024 px it is 2,695 texels/m, about 1:1 with 2160p at 0.5 m. Held or inspected books closer than 0.5 m need more. Recommend **512x512 for a full cover face (or the cover+spine strip), 1024 only if a hero inspect view needs it**. **[derived]**
- A 0.06 m spine at 1.5 m on 1080p is about 28 px wide; a rounded spine is invisible as a silhouette there, only its shading shows. **[derived]**
- Triangle budget **[derived from a construction, not a source]**: cross-section outline of about 14 vertices (front board, back board, spine arc of 4-5 segments) extruded the book's height: 14 side quads = 28 triangles; top and bottom caps as fans of 12 triangles each = 24; a page block as a box with 3 visible faces (top, bottom, fore-edge) = 6 triangles, or 12 with all faces. DXT1/BC1 (4 bpp, no alpha) halves that. 30 category colours x 4 patterns = 120 materials sharing one shader is fine for the SRP Batcher (file 10), but 120 textures only if each combination is baked.

## 5. Implication for BookBoy

Recommended pipeline, smallest sensible scope: **mesh and base patterns made procedurally in the repo; AI used (if at all) only to draw 3 to 6 neutral cover patterns; spine letters/numbers stay in-game; record provenance for anything AI-made.**

1. **Mesh: write a C# Editor generator (no Blender).** One parametric function `BuildBook(height, depth, thickness, spineRadius, padding)` producing the shell described in section 3 (front/back boards plus a rounded spine arc, an inset page block, split vertices so the spine arc is smooth and the board edges are hard, UV0 per the layout below, tangents only if a normal map is used). Save into `Assets/_Project/Art/Meshes/` as an asset, one per size class (the project has a handful of size classes; this is **UNSOURCED GUESS** about the count), refilled in place rather than recreated (rule 14). Reason to prefer this over Blender or any AI: no new software, no package change (CLAUDE.md guardrail), exact sizes so the BoxCollider and mesh agree, and Claude can edit and regenerate it. Keep Blender+bpy as the fallback if hand-shaped details are wanted later; install Blender then (it is not present now).
2. **Axis, pivot and scale (decide once, document):** mesh authored at real size with `localScale = (1,1,1)`, **not** the current "unit cube scaled by size" which would squash the spine radius and page inset on non-uniform scale. Pivot: bottom-centre of the footprint, spine on the slot's front side, so `ShelfSlot.Accept` changes to `position = slot.position + forward * (depth/2)` with no `scale.y * 0.5` term (current code assumes a centred pivot). **Design recommendation, UNSOURCED GUESS**; do it inside the experiment, not in the live scene.
3. **UV layout:** cover as one horizontal strip (back board, spine, front board) in the left ~75% of the 0-1 UV square so a weave or leather grain runs continuously around the spine; page block mapped into a small separate strip of paper colour (right ~25%); keep a flat, low-detail rectangle on the spine reserved for the game-drawn glyphs so they stay readable (contrast guidance in file 18). All UV in one 512 texture.
4. **Cover materials:** start with **hand/procedurally-made or CC0 patterns** (ambientCG/Poly Haven cloth and leather are free, CC0, no disclosure). Use AI only if those do not give the look wanted, and then only for neutral, desaturated, tileable base patterns, with the category colour applied by the game. For the experiment keep `Mat_BookSpine`'s shader (URP Lit, Smoothness 0, Specular Highlights off, per file 09), put the pattern in Base Map, leave the normal map empty at first, add it only if the cover looks flat in the screenshot.
5. **Tint handling (the one real design choice):** the current `MaterialPropertyBlock` colour would tint the page block. For the experiment use two material slots on the book mesh (cover tinted via the `SetPropertyBlock(block, 0)` overload, paper untinted), accept two draws per book, and measure. If that is too many draws, switch (file 10 already advises leaving MPB) to per-colour materials or a vertex-colour-mask Shader Graph. Do not decide that from this document; measure Stats/Frame Debugger first, as file 10 says.
6. **Import settings:** textures sRGB for colour, mipmaps on, BC7/DXT5 for RGBA or DXT1 for opaque covers, Aniso Level to test at 2; meshes Read/Write off, no lightmap UVs (dynamic items), Normals Import (the generator supplies them).
7. **If AI is used for textures:** prefer a tool with explicit, documented commercial terms and tileable output; record per asset (section 2.5 list); mark pre-generated AI content in the Steam Content Survey; keep at least a human-edited, hand-arranged final (USCO). Do not use AI meshes for books. Do not use a model whose licence excludes EU/UK sales (Hunyuan3D 2.1). Resolve D-005 before any AI asset is committed.

### Tiny first experiment (T-004), human-checkable by rendered screenshot

Scope: one procedurally generated book mesh (one size class), 3 cover variants (e.g. plain cloth, leather grain, bordered pattern; made procedurally or from a CC0 set, so no AI and no disclosure yet), placed in a throwaway test scene or the existing prototype shelf; do not touch the Lamplit scene or the builder yet.

Set up: fixed camera, FOV 75, 1920x1080, same lights as the shelf; screenshots at 0.5 m, 1.0 m and 1.5 m; three books side by side plus one existing cube book as the baseline.

Acceptance criteria (thresholds are my proposals, **UNSOURCED GUESS**):
1. **Shape:** at 0.5 m the screenshot shows a rounded spine (smooth shading gradient across the spine), a visible lighter page block recessed from the cover edge on the fore-edge, and boards that meet the spine without gaps or flipped faces; at 1.5 m the book still reads as a book-shaped rectangle with a visible page-block edge.
2. **Triangles:** Stats shows 300 or fewer triangles per book (measure with one book alone), and total triangles for the 3-book scene grows by less than 1,000.
3. **Fit:** `Renderer.bounds.size` equals the intended size within 1 mm on each axis, and a side screenshot shows the book flush on the board top with its spine face on the front line (the `ShelfSlot.Accept` convention).
4. **Colour readability:** the cover's average colour, sampled from the screenshot at 1.0 m, is within a modest tolerance of the same colour on the baseline cube (threshold to set, e.g. hue within about 10 degrees, luminance within about 15%), and none of the three covers clips to near-white or near-black.
5. **Texture quality:** at 0.5 m the cover pattern is sharp, shows no visible seam where tiling or the spine wrap meets, and at 1.5 m shows no shimmer or moire (mipmaps on).
6. **Letters:** the existing spine glyph objects are still readable at 1.0 m on all three covers (compare to the cube baseline; contrast not worse).
7. **Cost:** Stats "Batches" and "Set Pass Calls" for the 3-book scene recorded and compared with the same count of cubes; texture memory per cover at or under 0.35 MB (512 BC7 with mips). Note whether the books appear under "SRP Batcher" or "Non-SRP Compatible".

Pass = all seven; the output of the experiment is also a measured tint-handling decision (point 5) and a provenance table row for each cover (even if "not AI"), which exercises the recording habit before any AI asset exists.
